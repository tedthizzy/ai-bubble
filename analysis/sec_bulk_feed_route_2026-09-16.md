# SEC daily bulk archive route, September 16, 2026

The SEC publishes one compressed tar Feed archive per filing day at `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/Feed/YYYY/QTRn/YYYYMMDD.nc.tar.gz`. Its [access guide](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) confirms the path and separately says Oldloads holds daily concatenated complete public submissions with filing headers. The SEC [PDS technical specification](https://www.sec.gov/files/edgar/pds_dissemination_spec.pdf) says a disseminated electronic submission contains the header and all public attached documents, including exhibits. The access guide does not explicitly state which PDS payloads appear inside a Feed tar member. Feed therefore remains a candidate route until a bounded real archive sample identifies member names and content.

The official [2026 Q2 Feed listing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/Feed/2026/QTR2/) contains 21 June 1-30 archives. Their displayed sizes sum to 27,662,870 KB, or 26.38 GiB when the displayed KB is treated as 1,024 bytes. The displayed daily range is 709,071-2,199,634 KB. The local 78,486-row selected SEC document manifest (`data/manifests/edgar_filing_manifest_20260917-001211.csv`) sums to 77.57 GiB of reported document sizes, with some documents already acquired separately. The Q3 Feed listing and September 16 archive availability remain unverified. An estimate from June's average over the 53 likely Q3 filing days through September 15 is 66.6 GiB for Q3, about 93 GiB from June 1 through September 15. This is a planning estimate, not an observed Q3 total. The Mac data volume had 180 GiB available when checked. Retaining all compressed archives and separately extracted documents would risk exhausting that space.

If the sample proves complete member payloads, stream each daily gzip/tar response through a sequential parser. Filter by accession and `<FILENAME>` against the dated manifest, retain only selected primary and exhibit text, and record source archive URL, tar member, direct SEC document URL, byte count, and SHA-256. Never extract arbitrary tar paths. Write a per-day completion record only after reaching the gzip end marker and reconciling member accessions to that day's SEC master index. An interrupted gzip day must be restarted; standard gzip does not offer practical member-level random access. The complete-file [quarterly and daily master indexes](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data) remain the filing denominator. SEC states that later removals are not retroactively reflected in old Feed or Oldloads indexes, while full and quarterly indexes are rebuilt weekly; the dated audit must reconcile this difference.

The route would reduce tens of thousands of document HTTP requests to roughly one request per filing day, but it would transfer all filings for each day, including irrelevant ones. The byte and elapsed-time tradeoff cannot be resolved from request counts alone. It is only a replacement for direct document and exhibit requests after a real Feed sample proves document coverage and a day-level reconciliation proves completeness. Use direct accession requests for missing or corrected documents. SEC's [fair-access policy](https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources) caps all agents together at 10 requests per second and asks scripts to download efficiently. The active acquisition processes currently consume 9.5 requests per second; no direct Feed request has been made pending a free lane.

## Live check at 22:00 Eastern on September 16

The pending checks above are now resolved in part. A direct SEC `Range: bytes=0-2097151` request for [the June 10 Feed archive](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/Feed/2026/QTR2/20260610.nc.tar.gz) returned HTTP 206 and `Content-Range: bytes 0-2097151/910920768`; the process read exactly 2 MiB and closed the response. The gzip prefix yielded a tar directory entry followed by accession-named `.nc` members. The first 16 complete sampled members contained `<SUBMISSION>` and `<DOCUMENT>/<TEXT>` payloads; three included four `EX-` exhibit-type sections each. One complete member contained 12 document sections. This proves the Feed holds public filing documents and at least some exhibits, so it is a viable bulk substitute for accession-level primary/exhibit GETs. A small prefix does not prove archive-wide completeness or one-to-one equivalence with direct SEC document bytes.

The live [Q3 directory JSON](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/Feed/2026/QTR3/index.json) listed 53 archives from July 1 through September 15. Displayed sizes summed to 69,929,069 KB = 66.69 GiB. Together with the 21 June archives, the observed available period contains 74 archives and 93.07 GiB compressed. The September 16 archive was absent at 22:00 Eastern; previous days in this directory were generally posted after 22:05 Eastern. The earlier Q3 estimate of 66.6 GiB is superseded by this measured 66.69 GiB listing. Downloading all 74 archives was outside this structural check. The exact completeness decision still requires streaming a whole day, checking the gzip trailer, and reconciling accessions and selected document filenames to the current master index and direct SEC filing directories.
