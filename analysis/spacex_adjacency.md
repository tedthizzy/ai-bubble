## Correction log (press → filing, 2026-06-13)

Reading the 424B4 directly **corrected three load-bearing press claims and added two uncarded commitments** — exactly the press-tier risk the original card flagged:

1. **The Google contract is not supported by the filing.** The original card carried a "Google $920M/month, 110k GPUs, ~$30B" compute contract sourced to a "June 5 S-1 amendment." The definitive 424B4 (filed June 12, *after* any June-5 document) contains **no such contract**: "Google" appears only as the Play Store, a Grok competitor, and Google Fiber; the only "$920M" is a 2024 share buyback. EDGAR's filing list shows **no June-5 S-1 amendment** (5/20 S-1 → 6/01 S-1/A → 6/03 S-1/A → 6/12 424B4). **The Google deal — and the "Alphabet 5% holder *and* $920M/mo customer" circularity built on it — is withdrawn pending any primary-source corroboration.** (Alphabet's ~5% equity stake is separately Alphabet-filing-derivable and may stand; the *customer* leg does not.)
2. **Termination is more aggressive than carded.** Not "after 2026-12-31" — the filing says terminable "**after the initial three-month period**" (the agreements were entered May 2026), i.e. from ~Aug 2026. The firm minimum is therefore *smaller* than the original card computed.
3. **Capacity/GPU figures corrected:** ~**325,000** NVIDIA GPUs across **Colossus *and* Colossus II** (~1.0 GW combined), not "220k+ in Colossus 1."
4. **New (uncarded) commitments:** a **Cursor (Anysphere)** compute-and-option agreement (Apr 2026) — option to acquire Cursor at ~$60B implied, **$1.5B termination fee + $8.5B deferred-services** obligation; and **Terafab**, a chip-manufacturing initiative with Tesla and Intel.

## The forensic read (same lenses we applied to the cluster)

**1. Headline vs. firm — the same shape as the engine's 98% strip, now filing-verified and *sharper*.** The verified committed contract is the Anthropic agreement: **$1.25B/month through May 2029** ≈ **~$45B gross** (~36 months, first two at a reduced ramp fee). But the filing makes it cancellable far sooner than the press implied: "**after the initial three-month period, the agreements may be terminated by either party upon 90 days' notice.**" The agreements were entered May 2026, so the earliest effective termination is roughly: initial 3-month period (≈ through Jul 2026) + 90 days' notice ⇒ **~end-Oct 2026**. Termination-adjusted **firm minimum ≈ 6 months** (May–Oct 2026, two of them ramp) ≈ **~$5–6B** — i.e. **~$5–6B firm against ~$45B gross, an ~87% haircut** before any view on renewal, and *steeper* than the original press card's $18B/76% (which double-counted a now-withdrawn Google contract and assumed a later termination date). Arithmetic is ours; the exact start of the three-month clock and whether notice may be served *at* vs *after* the initial period are the remaining flagged unknowns. This is the take-or-pay-vs-cancellable distinction that separated the cluster's verified backlog from its claimed paper — here, the "backlog" is ~90%-cancellable within a year.

**2. Circularity — the Alphabet instance is withdrawn; the live ones are NVIDIA, Cursor, and Anthropic itself.** The card's headline "customer-as-shareholder" instance (Alphabet 5% holder *and* $920M/mo customer) **does not survive the filing** — the customer leg isn't in the 424B4 (§correction log). What the filing *does* show: (a) **NVIDIA** as the hardware inside the Anthropic deal (~325,000 GPUs); (b) a **Cursor** compute-and-option structure where SpaceX is simultaneously Cursor's compute *supplier* and holds an *option to acquire* it (~$60B implied, $1.5B break fee, $8.5B deferred services) — supplier-and-acquirer in one counterparty; and (c) **Anthropic** appearing on both sides of our map (cluster ties + this lease), so a frontier-lab funding stumble propagates into two compute ecosystems. The circularity pattern holds; the specific largest instance was a press artifact and is retracted.

**3. Narrative vs. signed reality.** The valuation narrative is **orbital** (1M-satellite FCC filing, AI1 unveiling four days before listing, "multiple gigawatts" of orbital capacity under *consideration* by Anthropic). The signed revenue is **terrestrial GPU leasing** — Colossus + Colossus II in Memphis/Southaven and a conventional, cancellable cloud-services agreement. The $474B/2030 roadshow projection rests on execution physics (launch cadence, on-orbit power/thermal/radiation, ground-link bandwidth) for which no delivery evidence exists yet. Money is arriving ahead of physical proof — the pattern the engine documents, here in its purest form.

**4. Demand-leg concentration, now spanning providers.** Anthropic's $15B/yr commitment to SpaceX compares to a company-stated **$47B run-rate** (2026-05-28 Series H announcement) — roughly **a third of its run-rate committed to one compute provider** *(corrected 2026-06-12: this card originally cited the April 6 ~$30B print as current, implying "roughly half")*, while Anthropic remains capital-markets-funded (the same funding class as OpenAI in our graph, where OpenAI = 56% of CoreWeave's named backlog). Anthropic now appears on both sides of our map: cluster ties (Hut 8/Google) and the SpaceX lease. A funding stumble at either frontier lab now propagates into *two* compute ecosystems.

**5. Classification — and why the cluster verdict does not move.** SpaceX/SPCX buckets as **pure-equity narrative**, not `financed_ai_infra_leveraged`: it just raised $75B of primary equity, carries no disclosed AI-infra debt burden, and has no debt-service coverage to breach. There is no Minsky solvency mechanic here — the risk axis is **expectations** (what $2T implies vs. terminable contracts and unproven orbital economics), not insolvency. Scope discipline therefore holds: this card is **pattern-extension evidence** for the ecosystem question — circular validation, narrative-forward valuation, soft-contract headline inflation — and the ecosystem verdict stays capped at 0.25 until evidence of this class passes the gate at volume.

**6. Cluster interaction (watch item).** The verified Anthropic contract puts **~$15B/yr of compute supply** ($1.25B/mo) into the market from a $75B-capitalized competitor with zero debt-service pressure — direct competition for the renewals and utilization the leveraged cluster *must* win to service debt. A well-capitalized entrant that can price below cost indefinitely is a new bear input for cluster utilization assumptions. (The figure is down from the original card's ~$26B/yr, which included the withdrawn Google contract.)
