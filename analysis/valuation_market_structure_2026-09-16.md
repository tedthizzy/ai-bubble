# AI equity valuation and financing concentration, September 16, 2026

The valuation evidence has become more bubble-like since the June baseline, even while recognized AI sales have grown. Two private model developers reported very large preferred financing rounds against annualized revenue paces. Public suppliers and landlords report real sales and contracted backlog, but much of that backlog ultimately depends on a small group of model labs and cloud buyers. Public equity concentration is high. None of these observations establishes that a public share-price collapse or a broad credit event has begun.

**Arithmetic correction to the Anthropic ratio below:** $965 billion / $47 billion = 20.5319, so a revenue pace greater than $47 billion implies a ratio below 20.532 times, approximately 20.5 times. The exact ratio could still exceed 20.5 times. The earlier phrase "below 20.5 times" is too strict after rounding.

**Later reported pace:** [Bloomberg's August 18 original report](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-surpasses-65-billion-ahead-of-ipo) puts Anthropic above a $65 billion annualized pace at the end of July, citing people familiar with an investor update. [Axios reports](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-revenue-run-rate-ipo-openai) an OpenAI internal message placing its pace above $40 billion in August. These later private-company figures are secondary-source reports and have different gross/net revenue treatment. They do not revise the contemporaneous March and May financing-round ratios below or provide comparable cash flow.

[OpenAI's March 31 announcement](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/) says its round had $122 billion of **committed capital** at an $852 billion post-money valuation. The same announcement says it was generating $2 billion of revenue per month at that date. Multiplying that one-month pace by 12 gives a $24 billion annualized pace, making the stated post-money mark about 35.5 times that pace. This is a ratio of a private financing mark to a point-in-time, unaudited company revenue statement. The announcement does not establish that all committed capital was already funded, the economics of each security class, audited trailing sales, or an observable September 16 public market capitalization.

[Anthropic's May 28 Series H announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h) says it raised $65 billion at a $965 billion post-money valuation and crossed **$47 billion annualized revenue** earlier that month. Because it says *more than* $47 billion, $965 billion divided by the disclosed lower bound is about 20.5; the actual mark-to-stated-pace ratio is **below 20.5 times**, subject to both numbers' definitions. The $65 billion round includes $15 billion of previously committed hyperscaler investments, including $5 billion from Amazon. Those earlier commitments must not be added to the round again. A private preferred mark is not a transaction price for all common shares, and the announcement is not an audited full-year revenue statement. These two disclosures support an elevated valuation-risk finding without furnishing a comparable September stock-market multiple for either lab.

There is direct evidence of supplier-investor links. [Amazon's June 30 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm) reports $28.7 billion of OpenAI equity purchased in the first half and another $21.3 billion funded after June 30, plus $10.0 billion of Anthropic preferred purchased in Q2. It also reports a $100 billion first-quarter expansion of its existing OpenAI AWS arrangement and a more-than-$100 billion second-quarter expansion of its Anthropic AWS arrangement. These are equity purchases and model-lab cloud purchase commitments, respectively; their contractual amounts are not earned Amazon revenue or independent proof of third-party customer profit. The filing does not trace an invested dollar to a specific invoice, so the cash-flow loop is an exposure mechanism rather than a proven dollar-for-dollar circular transaction.

The private marks already transmit into a public company's accounts. The same [Amazon 10-Q investment note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm) values its Anthropic nonvoting preferred at $92.5 billion and Anthropic convertible notes at $97.9 billion as of June 30. The notes had $92.0 billion of associated unrealized gain in accumulated other comprehensive income. Amazon booked a $50.5 billion Q2 upward adjustment to the preferred shares after observable Anthropic funding prices; its $53.4 billion total Q2 other income was primarily Anthropic valuation adjustments and conversion-related reclassifications, exceeding its $27.461 billion Q2 consolidated operating income. These are accounting marks, not AWS sales or cash collected from AI users. Amazon reports $229.7 billion total recorded equity, warrant, and convertible-debt investments, of which only $7.4 billion was public equity and warrants, and explicitly describes private-company valuation as more complex because market data are scarce. The preferred and notes are different holdings of one issuer; their carrying values should not be mistaken for Anthropic's own cash balance or added to its reported round.

[NVIDIA's July 26 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) reports $99 billion of ecosystem equity investments and $25 billion of equity-investment commitments, without assigning all of them to model labs. The same filing reports $36 billion of AI-cloud service commitments under agreements in which partner clouds buy NVIDIA infrastructure and NVIDIA agrees to buy capacity that the partners can resell to other customers. Its August SB Energy agreement caps a phased guarantee at $105 billion for leases to OpenAI; the first lease commencements are expected in fiscal 2029. These are three different balance-sheet or contingent exposures, and the $105 billion is a maximum future guarantee rather than outstanding debt. NVIDIA generated $74.421 billion of operating cash flow in its fiscal first half, so the financing ties coexist with substantial current cash generation.

Payment timing and customer concentration make the financing ties consequential. [NVIDIA's Q2 CFO commentary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27cfocommentary.htm) reports $63.1 billion of accounts receivable and 60 days sales outstanding, up from 45 days in the prior quarter, attributing the change to extended payment terms on large agreements with certain investment-grade customers. Its [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) says five direct customers represented 22%, 14%, 13%, 11%, and 10% of receivables as of July 26, a combined 70%; one direct customer represented 16% of Q2 revenue. The filing also estimates that one unnamed AI research and deployment company contributed a meaningful amount of indirect revenue through NVIDIA customers. The direct and indirect categories overlap economically, and that company cannot be identified from this disclosure. Longer receivable terms are a working-capital signal, not by themselves proof of nonpayment.

The cloud-to-landlord chain is similarly narrow. [CoreWeave's Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000366/crwv-20260630.htm) reports $103.7 billion of remaining performance obligations at June 30, with 41% expected over the next 24 months, and says committed contracts generated 98% of Q2 recognized revenue. Three anonymized customers accounted for 36%, 26%, and 10% of Q2 revenue. The same filing says its two largest named-by-code customers each accounted for 32% of June 30 receivables. [Core Scientific's Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839341/000183934126000014/core-20260630.htm) says all its colocation revenue came from CoreWeave and represented about 77% of Core Scientific's first-half total revenue. This establishes concentration in two adjacent public companies; it does not identify CoreWeave's anonymized end customers or make the two reported revenues additive final demand.

[Cerebras's Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2021728/000162828026056357/cbrs-20260630.htm) provides a third route through the same buyers. It reports $25.4 billion of remaining performance obligations, a significant amount from its OpenAI agreement, and $74.4 million first-half recognized revenue under that agreement. OpenAI's $1 billion secured working-capital loan supported the capacity build; $918.2 million remained outstanding at June 30. OpenAI warrants generated an $822.9 million customer-warrant asset in first-half 2026, to be charged against future revenue. Those facts make contract value, financing, equity consideration, and GAAP revenue distinct measurements of one relationship. Cerebras completed its May IPO and reported $6.2 billion net proceeds, so the company also has a public financing route; an IPO price alone would not establish its September 16 market value.

Financing costs show that access to capital does not mean inexpensive funding. [CoreWeave priced $1.25 billion of 9.625% senior unsecured dollar notes at par on June 11](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Announces-Pricing-of-1-25-Billion-of-Senior-Notes-and-2-Billion-of-Senior-Notes/default.aspx), alongside a separate EUR 2 billion 8.500% tranche. [Galaxy Helios II priced $3.507 billion of 9.875% senior secured notes on July 23](https://investor.galaxy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/galaxy-digital-inc-announces-pricing-3507-billion-senior-secured); [Galaxy later reported the issue completed July 28](https://investor.galaxy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/galaxy-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-results). Galaxy's proceeds fund a project with 400 MW utility capacity and 260 MW critical IT capacity, two different denominators. The notes have different collateral, currencies, maturities, and project risks, so their coupons are examples of financing terms, not a uniform AI borrowing rate.

[S&P Dow Jones Indices' S&P 500 characteristics](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/equity/sp-500/) put its largest constituent at 8.1% and top ten constituents at 37.8% as of August 31. NVIDIA was the largest constituent in the displayed roster. [S&P's May 2026 historical review](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/research/article/in-the-shadows-of-giants/) says the ten largest **companies** had already reached almost 40% by mid-2025. The current metric counts listed share classes as constituents, while the historical description counts companies, so they do not establish a precise change in concentration. Both show that public equity concentration was already high before September 2026. The top-ten basket also includes companies and revenue streams unrelated to AI. This broad-index observation cannot price private preferred shares or prove that all public mega-cap valuations are an AI bubble.

The practical verdict is narrower than an all-market bubble call. Private model-lab marks and linked supplier financing have grown into a large, concentrated valuation and funding risk. Recognized cloud, chip, and AI-service sales are substantial, and NVIDIA and Microsoft still generate large positive operating cash after their own asset purchases. Current public filings do not isolate the ultimate customer cash returns needed to underwrite the full long-dated contract stack. The repo's registered September credit test has one confirming and one contra signal, so these valuation observations should change the qualitative risk assessment without being laundered into a new calibrated crash probability.
