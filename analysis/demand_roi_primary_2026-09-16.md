# AI demand and ROI: primary-source audit, September 16, 2026

Demand is materially stronger than the June snapshot. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, SpaceX and Broadcom disclose substantial usage, sales, or both. The return on the whole infrastructure buildout remains unresolved. Much of the reported cloud and chip revenue is a cost to a model lab, while the lab's own revenue is an annualized exit rate rather than a filed full-year result. The same dollar can appear at several points in the supply chain. The video transcript's claim that "nobody has measured" customer ROI is too broad: measured workflow results and randomized trials exist. They do not establish aggregate enterprise returns or a return on 2027 compute commitments.

This audit uses material published by September 16, 2026. "Recognized revenue" means revenue reported for a completed accounting period. "Run-rate revenue" annualizes a recent pace and is not full-year recognized revenue. "RPO" is revenue contracted for future recognition subject to the issuer's accounting definition, delivery and any contractual rights. Usage, seat, and contract counts are different units. A vendor case study is evidence for its named workflow, not an estimate for all customers. All money amounts below are USD.

## Actual demand disclosures

- **OpenAI.** Its [March 31 funding announcement](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/) says it was generating $2 billion of revenue per month at that time, equivalent to a $24 billion annualized pace, and that enterprise represented over 40% of revenue. It disclosed more than 900 million weekly ChatGPT users and 50 million consumer subscribers at that date. Its [September 8 update](https://openai.com/index/the-work-now-within-reach/) says products reached more than one billion weekly active users and 2.5 million businesses. Its [August 31 advertising announcement](https://openai.com/index/expanding-access-to-ai-with-chatgpt-ads/) says ChatGPT Ads reached a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate and tens of thousands of advertisers. The ads figure is a component of OpenAI revenue, not an increment to be added to the March total. OpenAI has not provided in these checked company disclosures a 2026 GAAP year-to-date revenue, operating profit, or cash-flow statement. The March pace is five months older than the ad figure; neither proves a full-year 2026 revenue forecast.

- **Anthropic.** Its [May 28 Series H announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h) states that revenue run rate crossed $47 billion earlier in May. The [April 6 company update](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute) said the run rate had surpassed $30 billion, up from about $9 billion at end-2025, and more than 1,000 business customers each represented over $1 million of annualized spend. Its [Amazon agreement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) says more than 100,000 customers use Claude on Bedrock and commits more than $100 billion to AWS technologies over ten years for up to 5 GW of *future* capacity. The $47 billion is an unaudited annualized company statement, not 2026 recognized revenue, free cash flow, or proof that all future capacity has paying end users. Bedrock customers and Anthropic customers may overlap, and Bedrock sales are part of AWS's revenue chain.

- **Alphabet/Google.** Alphabet's [July 22 Q2 release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) reports $24.768 billion of Q2 Google Cloud recognized revenue, up 82% year over year, and $8.814 billion of Cloud operating income. It attributes Cloud growth to AI infrastructure, AI solutions, and core GCP services. It reported 950 million Gemini App monthly active users as of that release; a separate [August 11 Google announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/one-billion-monthly-users/) says the app exceeded one billion monthly active users. Monthly Gemini users cannot be compared directly with weekly ChatGPT users. The Cloud segment includes Workspace, infrastructure and TPU system sales, and Alphabet reports shared AI research costs outside the Cloud segment. Consequently, neither total Cloud revenue nor segment profit isolates the economics of Gemini model sales. The video's claim that Google is out of the frontier is a reported opinion about model quality, not a finding supported by these commercial disclosures.

- **Microsoft.** [FY2026 Q4 investor metrics](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/metrics) show $59.3 billion of Q4 Microsoft Cloud recognized revenue, $214.4 billion for the year, Azure and other cloud services growth of 43% in Q4, and $678 billion of commercial RPO. The [earnings call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4) says nearly 90% of annual Microsoft Cloud revenue came from customers outside frontier model companies, commercial RPO grew 25% excluding OpenAI, and about 30% of total commercial RPO is scheduled for recognition in the next 12 months. It reports over 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats and 100,000 Foundry customers; the former is paid access, while engagement is separately described as increasing. Microsoft's Azure/Foundry revenue can include model-lab purchases and resale of other labs' APIs. Neither Cloud total nor Copilot paid-seat count is a standalone AI revenue figure. The $678 billion RPO is not current-year revenue or an end-customer ROI measure.

- **Amazon.** Its [July 30 Q2 release](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q2-2026-report) reports $42.2 billion of AWS Q2 recognized net sales, up 37% year over year, and $16.6 billion of AWS operating income. It says the AWS AI business and chips business *each* exceeded $25 billion annualized revenue run rates, and Bedrock has hundreds of thousands of customers. The two run rates have undefined perimeter and may overlap each other and the $42.2 billion AWS quarter; they cannot be summed as two independent end-demand streams. The same release says Amazon's $62.6 billion Q2 net income included $53.4 billion of non-operating pretax other income, primarily from its Anthropic investment. That investment revaluation is separate from AWS recognized sales and is not a customer productivity gain.

- **Oracle.** Its [September 10 FY2027 Q1 release](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2026/Oracle-Announces-Q1-Results-Driven-by-Triple-Digit-Growth-in-Cloud-Infrastructure-Revenues/default.aspx) reports $7.4 billion of quarterly Cloud Infrastructure recognized revenue, up 121%, within $19.3 billion of total revenue. It reports $664 billion RPO after adding more than $30 billion of AI cloud contracts during the quarter, and says over 300,000 GPUs were delivered to AI cloud customers since the previous quarter. The release also reports about $5 billion negative free cash flow for Q1. Recognized IaaS growth is real, but the RPO is a multi-year contract stock, the GPU count is delivery rather than utilization, and neither isolates inference revenue from training or non-AI cloud.

- **Meta.** Its [July 29 Q2 release](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) reports $60.801 billion of recognized total revenue, up 28%, with ad impressions up 14% and average price per ad up 12%. Q2 operating income fell 8% to $18.775 billion while capital spending including finance-lease principal reached $31.08 billion. In the [Q2 call transcript](https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/META-Q2-2026-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf), management says AI-powered Advantage+ end-to-end ad solutions exceeded a $75 billion annual revenue run rate and over 9 million small businesses use at least one AI creative tool. Advantage+ is part of the existing advertising business, not $75 billion of separate generative-AI fees. The customer example of a 13% incremental purchase lift is a named campaign result and cannot identify the AI contribution to Meta's total ad growth.

- **SpaceX/xAI.** SpaceX's [August 4 Q2 earnings release](https://s21.q4cdn.com/184289198/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/SpaceX-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.pdf) reports $2.194 billion of recognized AI Solutions & Infrastructure quarterly revenue and $367 million advertising revenue, within $2.561 billion of AI segment revenue. New Cloud Services Agreements contributed $1.6 billion of incremental Q2 infrastructure revenue. The AI segment recorded a $1.257 billion operating loss and $15.828 billion Q2 capital expenditure. It reports $14.1 billion of contracted sales, defined to include only the non-cancellable enforceable period and revenue already recognized while excluding cancellable future periods. That is stronger than a hypothetical full-term contract value but still future provider sales, not lab revenue or customer ROI. SpaceX's 1.4 GW nameplate compute is its total cluster capacity, not Anthropic's dedicated inference capacity. SpaceX does not break out Grok subscription/API revenue, external capacity fees, or advertising more finely within the AI solutions line. The [local issuer refresh](case_zero_financials_2026-09-16_v4.md) separately reconciles the SpaceX filing to period-matched cash flow.

- **Broadcom and financing to Anthropic.** [Broadcom's September 2 Q3 release](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announces-third-quarter-fiscal-year-2026-financial) reports $16.7 billion of AI semiconductor revenue, up 221% year over year, within $29.591 billion of total recognized revenue; Q4's $21.7 billion AI semiconductor figure is guidance. The [June 9 Broadcom/Apollo/Blackstone announcement](https://ir.apollo.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/630/broadcom-apollo-and-blackstone-establish-landmark) identifies a $35 billion initial XPV transaction to support more than 1 GW of Anthropic-related compute expected at Fluidstack sites. The >20 GW platform through 2028 is a design ambition, not deployed supply. [Apollo's announcement](https://ir.apollo.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/629/apollo-leads-35-billion-capital-solution-for-broadcom-ai) calls the $35 billion a multi-year draw commitment. More specifically, Apollo's [Q2 10-Q, note "Purchase Obligation Guarantee"](https://ir.apollo.com/sec-filings/content/0001858681-26-000040/0001858681-26-000040.pdf) says Apollo-managed WarehouseCo has the equipment purchase obligation; committed financing is expected to fund it through 2028; Athene Holding Ltd guarantees 15% of the outstanding purchase obligation on WarehouseCo default and has a separate 35% reimbursement agreement for any guarantee payments. Apollo recorded no guarantee liability because it judged payment not probable. The filing does not state that $35 billion was already funded, or provide a current outstanding balance or exact fund-by-fund exposure. Broadcom recognized chip revenue is an upstream transaction enabled by capital-provider funding; it cannot be added to Anthropic model revenue as independent final demand.

## What the ROI evidence measures

[OpenAI's September 16 analytics announcement](https://openai.com/index/how-to-connect-ai-usage-to-business-value/) explicitly separates usage and spend from task outcomes. It provides a way for enterprises to inspect active users, credits, task categories and selected outcome metrics. The article's sample dashboard figures are hypothetical. It cites named customer examples, including [1Password's September 8 case](https://openai.com/index/1password/): 20.9% improvement in its core engineering cohort's productivity measure and 10.9% lower median pull-request cycle time. Its $783,750 estimated annual engineering capacity value is a model: 50 active developers x $250,000 fully loaded annual labor cost x 20.9% observed change x 40% directional Codex attribution x 75% capacity realization. The 553% ROI uses that modeled benefit against cost. The case does not disclose a randomized no-Codex group, a counterfactual trend, or cash savings of $783,750. It establishes a measured local workflow change and a transparent, assumption-dependent ROI estimate.

A [randomized Microsoft Research/NBER field experiment](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/revisions/w33795.rev1.pdf) assigned Copilot access to half of 7,137 workers in 66 firms during September 2023-October 2024. The intent-to-treat group spent 1.3 fewer hours per week on email; frequent users spent 3.6 fewer hours, a selected subgroup result; documents were completed moderately faster and meeting time did not change significantly. This is causal evidence of a task-level benefit in the tested period and workplace context. It does not price the software, quality of output, displaced labor, or whole-firm profit. Anthropic's [January 2026 randomized skill study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills) found 17% lower quiz performance with AI assistance and no statistically significant speedup on its tested task, a counterexample to assuming every short task benefits.

[METR's early-2025 randomized trial](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) found experienced maintainers took 19% longer on 246 tasks when AI was allowed. Its [February 2026 follow-up](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) says later raw estimates suggest a possible speedup but are unreliable because developers who refused no-AI work selected out and concurrent-agent time was hard to measure. The early result is not a verdict on September 2026 models. Anthropic's [80% task-time figure](https://www.anthropic.com/research/estimating-productivity-gains) is a model's estimate from Claude conversations and excludes human verification or revisions outside the chat; it is not an observed customer ROI metric. Its [June Claude Code session study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise) analyzes about 400,000 sessions and task success, but its estimated task dollar value uses model judgments against freelance postings, not cash realized by customers.

A [2026 NBER survey of about 6,000 firms](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34836/w34836.pdf), using responses mainly collected through January 2026, found 89% of executives reported no labor-productivity effect from AI in their own firm over the preceding three years. Mean reported effect was about +0.29% over those three years. That is survey evidence about earlier deployment, not a causal estimate of what new agent tools achieved by September. It cautions against turning early adopter case studies into an economy-wide return estimate. The video is right that a population-level ROI on the current infrastructure cohort remains unmeasured in these sources.

OpenAI's [September 6 research update](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/) describes an internal "research intern" that completes bounded tasks under human direction. It reports 3.1 agent workdays for each human workday in its research organization in August, more experiments per active experimenter, and substantial human intervention on longer tasks. The report states that compute also grew, so its experiment trend does not identify the causal contribution of agents to research progress. It does document a major change in internal usage, without proving recursive self-improvement or commercially valuable breakthroughs.

## Implications and unresolved measurements

The strongest counter to a simple no-demand bubble is recognized growth at Google Cloud, AWS, Oracle IaaS, SpaceX compute services and Broadcom, plus paid Copilot seats and broad consumer/enterprise model usage. The strongest bubble mechanism is capital-intensive upstream revenue financed against concentrated model-lab demand whose earnings and cash conversion remain unverified. Amazon's $53.4 billion Q2 non-operating gain primarily on Anthropic and Microsoft's reported $3.2 billion gain on Anthropic are investment marks, separate from product operating revenue. Alphabet's [Q2 release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) reports a $98.0 billion non-operating gain primarily on equity securities without a counterparty breakout; do not attribute it to Anthropic. Accounting gains on lab stakes, provider rental receipts, chip sales, and lab run-rate revenue answer different questions.

To determine whether the 2027 buildout earns its cost, the missing observations are a current recognized-revenue and cash-flow series for OpenAI and Anthropic; the share of each cloud provider's AI revenue funded by model labs rather than independent enterprise budgets; gross margin after inference, training, depreciation and financing; actual 2027 available accelerator capacity and occupancy; realized end-customer outcomes net of software, infrastructure, integration and human-review costs; and the outstanding draw/exposure schedule of large equipment-financing vehicles. The public disclosures above do not identify a $50 billion or $100 billion annual lab-revenue yield per GW of inference. Nameplate GW, contracted GW, energized GW, and average utilized inference GW are separate denominators.
