# Semiconductor supply and the 2027 compute claim

Evidence cutoff: September 16, 2026. All dates below are calendar dates, not fiscal-year labels. A "gigawatt" needs a denominator: facility electrical capacity, installed IT nameplate power, and power draw of shipped accelerator servers are different quantities. A chip shipment or signed capacity agreement is not an energized, revenue-producing AI server.
The later supply-chain section quotes issuer fiscal-year buckets exactly as filed; those buckets should not be read as calendar-year delivery dates.

## The 15 GW to 45-55 GW claim

The video transcript supplied for this refresh estimates 15 GW of *usable AI data-center capacity* at end-2026 and 45-55 GW at end-2027, a 30-40 GW one-year increase. I could not reproduce those two global, AI-only, *usable* stock figures from the primary sources checked below. The video does not give a facility list, an IT-versus-facility definition, a shipment bridge, or a realized energization schedule for the estimate. Treat it as a scenario, not an observed baseline or a verified delivery forecast.

The [IEA's April 16, 2026 bottom-up report](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai) is the closest public global comparator. Its [methodology and data annex](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3179f7f8-01f6-4dd6-bffa-c9f7b73f1dc9/KeyQuestionsonEnergyandAI.pdf) models shipped equipment, utilization and cooling separately. It estimates **114 GW total facility capacity and 82 GW installed IT capacity in 2025**, across *all* data centers, with 2030 base-case estimates of 226 and 174 GW. It does not publish an equivalent year-end 2026 and 2027 AI-only "usable capacity" stock series in the public annex. The report says current HBM production could support **at most roughly 25 GW of AI-ready servers per year through 2027**, citing an analyst estimate, and its own base case adds roughly **20 GW of accelerated servers per year by 2030**. These are modeled annual server additions, not an audited installed-capacity ceiling. The IEA expects AI memory scarcity through at least late 2027 and separately names packaging, grid connections, transformers and gas turbines as constraints. [Direct PDF, pp. 20-25 and 104-108](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/3179f7f8-01f6-4dd6-bffa-c9f7b73f1dc9/KeyQuestionsonEnergyandAI.pdf).

The [Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's June 2026 U.S. update](https://bies.lbl.gov/publications/united-states-data-center-energy-2025) is another bottom-up check. Its **2030 U.S. electricity-use range is 521-843 TWh**, driven in part by different assumptions for specialized accelerator installations, chip life, idle power and utilization. That energy range is not a 2027 global AI capacity count, but it demonstrates why nameplate MW alone cannot establish energy use or revenue.

The newer [SemiAnalysis September 15, 2026 first-party model summary](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/everyone-says-datacenter-moratoriums) forecasts **38 GW of U.S. data-center IT capacity delivered in 2027**, including 22 GW currently under vertical construction and 16 GW planned. That covers *all* data-center IT capacity in one country, including halls that may lack AI chips; it is not a global count of energized AI compute. Its facility-level model and validation records are subscription products, so the public summary cannot independently reproduce the 38 GW. The two forecasts can both describe an oversupply of powered halls relative to installed accelerators without either proving a 2027 glut of usable AI compute. [SemiAnalysis model description](https://semianalysis.com/models-research/).

## Packaging and memory: shipped versus scheduled

- **TSMC, producing:** Its April 23, 2026 technology release says **5.5-reticle CoWoS is in production**. The same release schedules **14-reticle CoWoS for 2028** and a **40-reticle SoW-X system-on-wafer variant for 2029**. Its COUPE co-packaged-optics solution was *scheduled* to begin production in 2026; this source does not establish customer deployment. A [2025 TSMC roadmap](https://pr.tsmc.com/static/campaign/2025august/tsmc_na/quarter.html) had targeted 9.5-reticle CoWoS and an SoW-X offering for 2027. The newer release changes the named SoW-X timetable/scope; it does not state whether the 2025 offering and 2026 40-reticle variant are identical. [TSMC 2026 release](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3302).
- **Panel packaging, tools available:** A [Nordson August 24, 2026 announcement hosted by SEMI](https://expo.semi.org/taiwan2026/Public/eBooth.aspx?BoothID=651104&Nav=False) introduced a dispenser for large panels, with glass-panel sensing and warpage controls. The equipment release demonstrates supply-chain preparation. It does not establish TSMC customer chips produced on glass panels.
- **Samsung, shipping:** Samsung announced commercial HBM4 mass production and shipment on **February 12**. It began HBM4E *sample* shipments on **May 29**. Its [July 30 Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results) say memory remained capacity-constrained, with rising industry prices, and expect H2 undersupply despite production increases. Samsung forecast HBM sales more than tripling in 2026, but did not publish shipped HBM stack counts or a global GW conversion. [HBM4 release](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-ships-industry-first-commercial-hbm4-with-ultimate-performance-for-ai-computing); [HBM4E samples](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-begins-shipment-of-industry-first-hbm4e-samples).
- **SK hynix, shipping:** Its [July 29 Q2 results](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/) report HBM4 *mass shipments* beginning in Q2, HBM4E samples in H1, and demand exceeding available supply. The first Yongin cleanroom is targeted for **February 2027**; a cleanroom opening is not volume HBM output. The Indiana advanced-packaging facility, which broke ground in August, targets **H2 2029** production. [Yongin investment update](https://news.skhynix.com/en/new-facility-investment-for-yongin-semiconductor-cluster/); [Indiana announcement](https://news.skhynix.com/en/groundbreaking-ceremony-in-indiana/).
- **Micron, shipping:** Its [June 24 FYQ3 release](https://investors.micron.com/news/press-release/2026/Micron-Technology-Inc--Reports-Record-Results-for-the-Third-Quarter-of-Fiscal-2026/default.aspx) says HBM4 is in high-volume shipments for a lead platform, while HBM4E volume production is expected in **2027**. The [prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf) report over **$1 billion of HBM4 shipments** to date and expect DRAM/NAND tightness beyond 2027. First wafers from Idaho ID1 are targeted for mid-2027; Singapore HBM packaging should contribute meaningfully in H1 2027. Sixteen take-or-pay strategic customer agreements cover about 20% of projected DRAM and one-third of NAND volume through 2030, reducing freely available future supply; those include products beyond HBM. Micron reported low-60s-percent sequential DRAM price growth and mid-80s-percent NAND price growth in FYQ3, both affected by product mix; these are not HBM-specific spot quotes.

These sources verify HBM4 production at all three memory vendors. None discloses a reconciled global HBM wafer-to-stack-to-server shipment series or 2027 average selling price by HBM generation. The IEA's 25 GW figure is therefore an independent estimate, not a sum independently audited from the three vendors.

## Compute systems and performance per watt

- **NVIDIA:** Its [August 26 earnings-call transcript](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/content_files/TRANSCRIPT_-NVIDIA-Corp-NVDA-US-Q2-2027-Earnings-Call-26-August-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf) says Vera Rubin **production shipments began in August**. NVIDIA expects supply constraints at least through its *fiscal* 2028, which extends into calendar 2027; this is management guidance, not an industry-wide supply measurement. Its [May 31 release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/vera-rubin-full-production-agentic-ai-factory) says Spectrum-X co-packaged-optics switches were in production. Vendor throughput-per-megawatt comparisons depend on workload, system boundary and software.
- **AMD:** AMD said [Helios rack systems entered production on July 23](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1294/aai-2026-amd-delivers-full-stack-compute-for-the-agentic-ai-era). OpenAI expects its first Helios deployment in Q4 2026, accelerating in 2027. A separate [AMD-Anthropic agreement](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1292/amd-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-up-to-2-gigawatts-of-amd-instinct-mi450-series-gpus) covers **up to 2 GW**, with the first gigawatt's deployment **beginning** in H1 2027. These are planned deployments, not 2 GW live today.
- **OpenAI custom silicon:** [OpenAI and Broadcom's June 24 Jalapeño announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/) identifies engineering samples running lab workloads and targets initial deployment by end-2026, then a multi-generation gigawatt-scale ramp. [OpenAI's August 25 results](https://openai.com/index/jalapeno-first-results/) report 1.5-1.9x peak work per watt and 1.7-3.6x lower latency than selected commercial systems on three public models. Those are company-run benchmark results using chip *rated* power for normalization; OpenAI also says production qualification and scale validation continue. They demonstrate a plausible efficiency shift, not a measured deployed-GW stock.
- **Other custom silicon:** [Google announced TPU 8t/8i on April 22](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/ai-infrastructure-at-next26); its [current product page](https://cloud.google.com/tpu) still lists both as "coming soon" and seventh-generation Ironwood as generally available. [Anthropic said on April 20](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) it was already using more than one million AWS Trainium2 chips, expected nearly 1 GW of Trainium2/3 capacity by end-2026, and contracted *up to* 5 GW with Amazon over ten years. That 5 GW is a contract ceiling over time, not current installed capacity.

## Supply-chain revenue, contracted output and financial support

Reported sales establish that suppliers are earning real revenue today, but they measure **sell-in to equipment makers and infrastructure buyers**, not end-user utilization or AI-service gross profit. NVIDIA reported **$89.0 billion** of Data Center revenue in its August 26 fiscal Q2 release; AMD reported **$6.7 billion** of Data Center revenue in its August 4 Q2 release, including EPYC CPUs as well as Instinct GPUs. Micron reported **more than $25 billion** of data-center revenue in its June 24 fiscal Q3 prepared remarks. TSMC reported **$40.20 billion total** Q2 revenue and **$15.70 billion** Q2 capex. SK hynix reported **KRW 79.3 trillion total** Q2 revenue, while Samsung reported **KRW 127.5 trillion** Q2 Device Solutions revenue. These segment definitions overlap along the same supply chain, so adding them would double-count end demand. [NVIDIA Q2 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx); [AMD Q2 results](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1295/amd-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results); [Micron remarks, p. 1](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf); [TSMC Q2 report, pp. 1, 6](https://investor.tsmc.com/japanese/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/qr/phase4_reports/2026-07/bf09036349a253e83f78155099133806cad0320b/2Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf); [SK hynix Q2 results](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/); [Samsung Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results).

**Contracted output differs from revenue.** Micron's 16 take-or-pay strategic customer agreements run mainly from calendar 2026 through 2030 and cover about **20% of projected DRAM volume and one-third of NAND volume** across data-center, consumer and automotive buyers. Fourteen agreements have about **$100 billion of cumulative minimum-price contract value** over the remaining term. Micron's GAAP remaining performance obligations were **over $5 billion at fiscal Q3 end** and roughly **$100 billion including agreements signed after quarter-end**. It projects **$22 billion of customer deposits and related financial commitments**, about **$18 billion** of that as cash deposits, which it says will be returned later and classified in financing cash flows. These amounts cannot be booked as 2027 HBM revenue or interpreted as cash paid by AI end users. SK hynix reports long-term agreements with around ten customers but gives no aggregate contract value or HBM unit schedule in its July release. Samsung's July release reports HBM4 sales growth without comparable contracted-unit disclosure. [Micron remarks, pp. 3, 7](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf); [SK hynix Q2 results](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/); [Samsung Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results).

**Supplier commitments and credit support add exposure beyond booked sales.** NVIDIA's August 27 company-hosted 10-Q lists **$279 billion of future supply-and-capacity commitments**, including **$92 billion in the remainder of fiscal 2027** and **$87 billion in fiscal 2028**; some supplier agreements can be canceled, rescheduled or adjusted before firm orders. It also lists **$36 billion of AI-cloud commitments**: those clouds buy NVIDIA infrastructure, while NVIDIA commits to buy their cloud services, subject to third-party substitution. NVIDIA separately guaranteed up to **$105 billion** of lease and power obligations for an OpenAI-affiliated tenant at SB Energy's proposed **4.25 GW IT-load** Ohio site. The guarantee is contingent on default and phased site completion; the first phase is expected in **fiscal 2029**. Another **$3.5 billion** covers guarantees for other AI clouds. These are maximum contingent exposures, not money already spent, equipment shipped, or 2027 usable capacity. [NVIDIA 10-Q, pp. 17-18](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf). AMD's [July 22 Anthropic announcement](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1292/amd-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-up-to-2-gigawatts-of-amd-instinct-mi450-series-gpus) likewise pairs a planned **up-to-2-GW** customer deployment beginning in H1 2027 with an AMD commitment to invest **up to $5 billion** in Anthropic equity. The investment is financing for a customer, not chip revenue.

NVIDIA also reported **$63.1 billion** of receivables at July 26, up from **$38.5 billion** at January 25, and **$74.4 billion** of operating cash flow for that fiscal half-year. It says some investment-grade customers receive **90-day to one-year** payment terms, so booked sell-in and cash collection can diverge. [NVIDIA 10-Q, pp. 5, 8, 14](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf).

This evidence strengthens both sides of the bubble test. Realized semiconductor sales and binding memory purchases support near-term demand. Multi-year supply purchases, refundable deposits, vendor cloud offtake and credit guarantees make reported pipeline figures poor proxies for independent end-user sell-through. The test is whether the installed systems earn enough external revenue and cash flow to carry their contracts once new supply arrives.

## Implication for the bubble test

The evidence supports rapid manufacturing and a credible 2027 pipeline of facilities and chips. It does **not** validate that 30-40 GW of additional *usable AI compute* will be online by December 2027. The most specific independent constraint estimate found is the IEA's roughly 25 GW/year HBM-supported server rate; the newer SemiAnalysis estimate suggests U.S. facility capacity alone may expand faster. That combination raises a concrete risk of stranded or underfilled data-center power contracts even while accelerators remain scarce. It does not establish a GPU-rental glut. A transition from shortage to glut requires tracking actual energized IT MW, installed accelerator MW, utilization and lease pricing by quarter, under one definition.

The video's fixed dollars-of-revenue-per-GW calculation should remain a sensitivity, because OpenAI, NVIDIA, AMD and Google are changing throughput per watt and the mix of training, inference and custom chips. Hardware-vendor revenue per shipped GW, infrastructure rent per available MW, and AI-lab revenue per inference MW are separate revenue streams; they cannot be multiplied into one another without utilization, price, contract term and power-boundary assumptions.

The [MLCommons Inference v6.1 release on September 16](https://mlcommons.org/2026/09/mlperf-inference-v6-1-results/) provides a dated performance cross-check. It reports a 5.7x improvement in the best per-accelerator server result for its DeepSeek-R1 test compared with v5.1 one year earlier and introduces an agentic-inference test. NVIDIA Rubin and Vera Rubin NVL72 entries are **preview**, while AMD MI350P and several other listed devices are available. [NVIDIA's submission note](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/vera-rubin-nvl72-mlperf-inference/) reports up to 3.7x greater Qwen3-VL throughput and 2.5x greater DeepSeek-R1 throughput for preview Vera Rubin NVL72 against GB300 NVL72 in specified configurations. These are benchmark throughput comparisons, not fleet-wide tokens per installed MW, energy consumption, utilization, price, or 2027 delivered server counts. They show why holding inference revenue per GW fixed through a hardware generation change is a weak assumption.
