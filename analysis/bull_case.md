## The bull thesis in one paragraph

The financed-compute cluster is not a 2007-style credit time bomb; it is project finance for a real, contracted industrial buildout. The debt is serviced not by spot GPU rental but by **take-or-pay contracts with the most creditworthy counterparties on earth**, the funding market is open and *discriminating* (not indiscriminate), the collateral question is mooted by demand outrunning supply through the decade, and every fragility the bears measure is either already disclosed (and therefore priced by sophisticated buyers reading the same filings) or carries an embedded hyperscaler backstop.

## The strongest arguments, in order

1. **The backlog is the balance sheet.** CoreWeave alone carries a ~$66B contracted revenue backlog — filing-verified take-or-pay from Microsoft, Meta ($14.2B through 2031), and OpenAI ($18.4B). FY2026 revenue guided to $12–13B (+~140%). The bears' zero-shock coverage math is a *snapshot during the capex ramp*; contracted revenue arriving 2026–2028 closes the coverage gap on schedule. Construction loans always look insolvent before the building opens.
2. **Customer concentration is underwriting, not just fragility.** The same fact the engine scores as existential — Microsoft at ~67% of CoreWeave revenue — means an investment-grade balance sheet with ~$100B+ annual operating cash flow has made itself the senior economic sponsor of the cluster's anchor. Hyperscalers cannot let strategic capacity fail in 2026–2027 when compute is their binding constraint; the realistic downside is *acquisition or contract cure*, not disorderly default. (CoreWeave acquiring Core Scientific shows consolidation absorbing weak credits already.)
3. **The funding market is doing diligence, not sleepwalking.** The June 2026 SPV print priced at 7.5% — *inside* the parent's 9.75% corporate level — because lenders underwrote the specific collateral and contracts and liked them. Euro books 3x+ oversubscribed. A-rated data-center ABS spreads have *compressed* over two years of heavy issuance. The dispersion in BDC discounts (ARCC ~par, FSK −41%) shows credit markets discriminating ruthlessly — which is what a functioning, informed market looks like, not a mania.
4. **GPU economic life is being extended by the inference cascade.** The 2–3yr economic-life estimate is a training-frontier number. Depreciated trainers redeploy to inference, fine-tuning, and batch workloads at positive contribution margin; scarcity of new supply (power, packaging, HBM) props the used market. If realized economic life is 4–5yr, the duration-mismatch leg of the bear case substantially closes.
5. **Power scarcity is the issuers' moat.** Energized, interconnected capacity — not GPUs — is the scarce asset. The cluster's sites, queue positions, and PPAs are appreciating assets that backstop refinancing even if chip collateral decays. The 2030–33 wall refinances against *real estate + power + contracts*, not against dead silicon.
6. **The macro tape disagrees with the bears.** Broad HY OAS at 2.78% is near record tights; the AI-debt market more than doubled its issuance run-rate with institutional demand. If the most informed credit investors in the world — reading the same public filings the engine reads — wanted out, the window would not be 3x oversubscribed.
7. **The bears' own engine concedes the boundary.** The adjudicated verdict caps the bubble at a ~4% leveraged tail of the AI-infra universe, ecosystem confidence 0.25, loss channel mostly parent-recourse equity wipeout rather than SPV fire-sale contagion. Even fully realized, the bear case is a contained sector event, not a systemic one — and contained events get absorbed by exactly the backstops in (2).

## What would prove the bull right (dated, observable)

- Cluster issuers keep printing 5yr+ paper at ≤~600bp over UST with >2x books **through 2026-Q4** (signal S1 stays *contra*).
- BDC discounts close back under ~10% across the top four as redemption pressure abates (S3 flips *contra*).
- CoreWeave FY2026 revenue lands ≥$12B with coverage inflecting positive ex-growth-capex by **2027-H1**.
- A visible secondary market for 2023–2024-vintage GPUs holds resale values above ~30% of cost at age 3 (kills the duration-mismatch leg).
- Anchor contracts renew or extend at the 2027–2028 checkpoints rather than shrinking (watch Microsoft commitments before the Oct-2030 OpenAI expiry).

## Where the bull case is weakest (so the steelman stays honest)

The bull must explain why the **funders' own investors** are exiting (FSK −41%, OBDC −22%, widespread redemptions) while the funders keep buying; why backlog quality survives if OpenAI — ~20% of CoreWeave's RPO per the FY2025 10-K ($11.9B of $60.7B; ~33% on the all-in press figure — the engine's stale 56% is corrected), and itself capital-markets-dependent — hits its own funding constraint; and why negative-carry issuers refinancing at 9.75% get healthier rather than deeper underwater while waiting for the backlog to convert. The bull answer to all three is "time and the window": it holds only if the window stays open. That is precisely what the pre-registered signals measure.
