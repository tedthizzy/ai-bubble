**The Asianometry video's glut scenario is plausible, but the arithmetic does not establish it.** The supplied transcript is the basis of this review. Its cited Dylan Patel interview provides estimates and forward assumptions, not an audited inventory. The interview and video also use capacity totals that cannot be reconciled without a common scope and definition. [Referenced interview](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/dylan-patel-3).

The key corrections are reproducible in [the notebook](refresh_2026-09-16.ipynb):

- `15 GW * $50B-$100B per GW-year = $750B-$1.5T per year` is the annual revenue rate if all 15GW is available and monetized for a full year. A year-end capacity assumption cannot establish calendar-2027 revenue.
- `40 GW * $20M per MW-year = $800B per year` is likewise an annual rental rate at full deployment. If those 40GW arrive uniformly through H2, they contribute an average of 10GW during 2027, implying $200B that year at the same price and full utilization. Uniform arrival through the whole year implies $400B. These examples exclude existing capacity, training allocation and any reservation payments before service; they correct the timing assumption rather than forecast actual spending.
- `$1.25B per month / 0.3 GW * 12 = $50B per GW-year` measures the infrastructure seller's receipts and the lab's expense. It does not measure the lab's revenue from customers. The transcript's monthly payment also needs reconciliation with the scope of the contract before it supports a unit price. Anthropic's May announcement describes 300MW-plus at Colossus 1, while the repo's older SpaceX filing note describes a broader arrangement. That prospectus could not be freshly retrieved in this pass. [Anthropic's capacity announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/higher-limits-spacex).
- Internally owned capacity does not generate the same external rental cash payment as third-party capacity. Supplier revenue, cloud revenue and model-lab revenue overlap along the same supply chain. Adding them overstates independent final demand.

The actual 2027 question is how much useful compute starts operating each month, at what utilization and price, against which financing obligations. Faster chips can expand effective supply without a proportional increase in power. Persistent agents can increase demand. Continued equity funding can sustain training expenses before matching customer revenue, so revenue falling short of an infrastructure estimate does not mathematically require an immediate rental-price collapse.
