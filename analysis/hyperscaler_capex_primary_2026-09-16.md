# Hyperscaler capital, commitments, cash, and backlog: September 16, 2026

The current filings show substantial AI infrastructure demand and a much larger long-duration obligation stack than a capex-only read suggests. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle each report large future contracts for equipment, cloud capacity, data centers, or power. The same filings show sharply different current cash conversion: Microsoft remains strongly cash-positive after cash capex, while Alphabet had a negative Q2 cash-after-capex quarter, Amazon had negative trailing-year free cash flow, Meta's Q2 free cash flow fell close to zero, and Oracle's August-quarter operating cash flow depended materially on customer prepayments. These observations measure the financing and timing of the buildout. They do not isolate AI project returns.

This audit uses disclosures available by September 16, 2026. All dollar amounts are USD. A capital expenditure is an asset purchase or lease addition under the company's definition; cash paid for property and equipment is a cash flow; an uncommenced lease is future payments under a signed lease that has not started; a purchase commitment is an agreed future spend; remaining performance obligation (RPO) is a customer's committed future revenue to the seller. These are different accounting objects. An obligation reported for one company can be a backlog item reported by its supplier. Dollar values below cannot be added across categories or issuers to estimate new final customer demand. Financing proceeds are liquidity, not sales. Uncommenced lease totals cover many years and should not be read as one-year rent or current debt.

- **Microsoft, fiscal year ended June 30, 2026.** The [FY2026 financial release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast) reports $182.935 billion of operating cash flow and $115.948 billion of cash additions to property and equipment, leaving $66.987 billion. Q4 alone was $55.441 billion and $35.802 billion, leaving $19.639 billion. The [earnings call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4) calls Q4 capex $41 billion, including $5.6 billion of finance leases; two thirds was short-lived equipment, primarily CPUs and GPUs. Its calendar-2026 capex expectation changed from about $190 billion in April to about $175 billion in July because a useful-life change shifts more future data-center leases from finance to operating lease classification. Management said the underlying calendar-2026 investment expectation was unchanged, so the lower reported capex guidance is not evidence of a lower buildout. The [FY2026 10-K, lease note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm) reports $329.1 billion of additional, primarily data-center leases not yet commenced; some are subject to contractual conditions, with expected commencements in fiscal 2027-2033 and terms of 1-20 years. The [call](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4) reports $678 billion commercial RPO, about 30% expected within 12 months; RPO includes OpenAI, and its weighted average duration is 2.3 years. It says all *sequential* RPO growth came from customers outside frontier model companies, but the existing RPO stock still includes frontier exposure.

- **Alphabet, quarter ended June 30, 2026.** Its [Q2 release and cash-flow statement](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) report $39.069 billion operating cash flow against $44.924 billion cash purchases of property and equipment, or negative $5.855 billion free cash flow. For first-half 2026, the corresponding figures are $84.859 billion and $80.598 billion, or positive $4.261 billion. Trailing-year free cash flow was $53.273 billion. The quarter also brought $49.6 billion net equity proceeds and $20.3 billion net senior-note proceeds, with the release citing AI infrastructure and global compute as uses for the equity proceeds. The [June 30 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204426000071/goog-20260630.htm) reports $811.0 billion material purchase commitments and other contractual obligations, including $200.7 billion short-term. Those obligations primarily cover technical infrastructure, inventory, content licenses and energy contracts; its $707.0 billion of fixed or guaranteed obligations on contracts over one year is a related subset, not another amount to add. Energy contracts can extend to 2054 and generally include take-or-pay minima and substantive termination fees. The same filing reports $519.5 billion revenue backlog, $513.9 billion of it Google Cloud, with just over half expected over 24 months; the timing depends on capacity delivery and customer usage. The filing also describes $7.6 billion maximum potential payments under financial guarantees, $43.8 billion under credit derivatives, an estimated $24.1 billion of future backstops subject to final terms, and a separate $20 billion contingent private-company funding commitment. The latter figures are contingent exposure or future funding terms, not current funded debt. Google Cloud's [Q2 release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) reports $24.768 billion revenue and $8.814 billion operating income, while noting shared AI research costs are allocated outside the Cloud segment.

- **Amazon, quarter ended June 30, 2026.** The [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm) reports $45.387 billion Q2 operating cash flow and $53.076 billion cash capital expenditure after property-sale proceeds and incentives, leaving negative $7.689 billion by that cash-capex convention. For the trailing 12 months, operating cash flow was $161.403 billion, net cash property purchases $169.007 billion, and free cash flow negative $7.604 billion. The 10-Q says most of its technology-infrastructure cash capex supports AWS, while fulfillment is also included; it does not provide an AI-only capex figure. Its footnote lists $137.214 billion of leases not yet commenced and $130.065 billion of unconditional purchase obligations, within a $650.034 billion schedule that also includes debt principal and interest, commenced leases, financing obligations, and other commitments. The $650.034 billion is a future-payment schedule, not incremental data-center spending. The same 10-Q reports about $496 billion of long-term customer performance obligations, primarily AWS, with a weighted average remaining contract life of 6.4 years. It identifies a $100 billion expansion to the existing OpenAI AWS arrangement in Q1 2026 and more than $100 billion expansion to Anthropic's AWS arrangement in Q2 2026, without reconciling each agreement to the $496 billion total. These are model-lab obligations to a supplier, not independent end-user revenue. The [Q2 release](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q2-2026-report) reports AWS revenue of $42.2 billion and $16.6 billion AWS operating income; the $53.4 billion Q2 non-operating gain, primarily on Anthropic, is excluded from those sales and operating-cash comparisons. The 10-Q reports $28.7 billion OpenAI equity purchases in first-half 2026 and a further $21.3 billion funded after June 30, plus $10.0 billion Anthropic preferred purchased in Q2; these are investments, not cash capex or AWS sales.

- **Meta, quarter ended June 30, 2026.** The [Q2 release](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) reports $31.862 billion operating cash flow and $31.078 billion of property purchases plus finance-lease principal, leaving $0.784 billion free cash flow. Its calendar-2026 capex outlook on the same definition is $130-145 billion. The [June 30 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm) reports $278.99 billion of data-center, colocation, and network leases not yet commenced, scheduled to begin through 2036 with terms up to 30 years. It discloses another approximately $68 billion of data-center leases signed in July 2026, after that balance-sheet date. Its $349.31 billion non-cancelable contractual commitments as of June 30 mainly involve third-party cloud capacity, servers, network equipment, data centers, and Reality Labs hardware, with $53.52 billion due during the remaining 2026 period and $81.65 billion in 2027. It also reports up to $14.72 billion of contingent cloud purchases reducible if the provider sells capacity to others, and $10.80 billion restricted money-market escrow securing infrastructure purchase agreements. The filing does not attribute all of these commitments to AI, and some obligations may reference the same project. It reported $24.91 billion net debt issuance in Q2 and $84.0 billion note principal outstanding at June 30. Its Q2 revenue is predominantly advertising; the [10-Q revenue note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm) reports $59.363 billion advertising revenue of $60.801 billion total, so current AI infrastructure return is not separately measurable from model fees.

- **Oracle, fiscal Q1 ended August 31, 2026.** The [Q1 release](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2026/Oracle-Announces-Q1-Results-Driven-by-Triple-Digit-Growth-in-Cloud-Infrastructure-Revenues/default.aspx) reconciles $23.103 billion operating cash flow with $28.499 billion cash capex, or negative $5.396 billion free cash flow. Its [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm) says $11.4 billion of the quarter's customer prepayments had significant financing components. Oracle's separate $17.966 billion 'net cash outlay for capital expenditures' starts with $28.499 billion gross capex, adds $0.830 billion of short-term capital-financing repayments, and subtracts $11.363 billion of customer prepayments. That financing view must not replace the GAAP cash-capex and free-cash-flow figures. The 10-Q reports $288 billion of additional, substantially data-center lease commitments not yet commenced, expected generally to commence from fiscal Q2 2027 through fiscal 2029 with terms of 15-19 years, plus $34.150 billion unconditional purchase and other obligations largely for cloud infrastructure components and power. It reports $664 billion RPO, with about 13% expected as revenue over the next 12 months, 37% in months 13-36, 34% in months 37-60, and the remainder later. Q1 recognized cloud infrastructure revenue was $7.4 billion. Oracle sold about $20 billion common equity in the quarter through an ATM program. Its [FY2026 release](https://investor.oracle.com/investor-news/news-details/2026/Oracle-Announces-Record-Q4-and-FY-2026-Results-Driven-by-Cloud-Infrastructure--Cloud-Applications/) says prepaid or customer-supplied hardware portions of large AI contracts total $75 billion. The issuer did not reconcile that hardware figure to RPO; it must not be added to RPO as separate final demand.

- **NVIDIA, fiscal Q2 ended July 26, 2026.** The [FY2027 Q2 10-Q](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf) reports $74.421 billion first-half operating cash flow and $4.434 billion purchases of property, equipment and intangibles, leaving $69.987 billion before investments and financing. It also reports $42.404 billion purchases of equity securities and $24.896 billion net debt proceeds in that half; equity purchases are separate from capex. The filing shows $279 billion future supply-and-capacity commitments, up from $119 billion the prior quarter, within a $366 billion commitment schedule that also includes $29 billion cloud-service agreements, $25 billion uncommenced leases, $25 billion equity-investment commitments and $8 billion capital commitments. Some supply arrangements can be cancelled, rescheduled, or adjusted before firm orders. A separate $36 billion AI-cloud service agreement schedule supports cloud partners that buy NVIDIA systems; the partners can resell capacity, so the hardware sale and NVIDIA's future cloud spend are economically linked. Its August SB Energy/OpenAI guarantee has a $105 billion maximum gross exposure for a phased 4.25 GW initial development, generally activating when each lease commences starting in fiscal 2029 and triggered by defined tenant defaults. The [10-Q](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/doc_financials/2027/NVDA-2027-Q2-10Q-Final-including-exhibits.pdf) also discloses an August memorandum of understanding with capital providers to *seek* more than $500 billion of third-party AI infrastructure capital over time; preliminary arrangements may not become definitive agreements. The $500 billion is neither funded capital nor NVIDIA revenue. Its [Q2 release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx) reports $96.221 billion total revenue and $89.0 billion Data Center revenue; those are chip and system supplier sales, not model-lab revenue or realized customer ROI.

Across these filings, the confirmed demand signal is recognized cloud and chip sales accompanied by multi-year customer contracts. The material financial risk is the duration and funding mismatch: future power, hardware, and lease obligations persist while customer usage, delivery timing, model-lab financing, and end-customer returns remain uncertain. The largest near-term cash-conversion deterioration is visible in Alphabet Q2, Amazon trailing 12 months, Meta Q2, and Oracle Q1. Microsoft and NVIDIA still generated substantial operating cash after their *own* cash asset purchases; NVIDIA also extended supply, equity, offtake and guarantee commitments. This evidence supports a bubble mechanism in financed infrastructure and concentrated counterparty exposure. It does not establish that every issuer will default, that contracts are all irrevocable, or that AI demand is absent.
