# Company tracking register, 2026-09-16

This register separates the June issuer cohort from companies added to the September SEC panel and the private inference hardware names raised in the September video. The [22-CIK SEC snapshot](case_zero_financials_2026-09-16_v4.md) contains filing dates, selected financial facts, and source URLs. A signed contract, deployed rack, recognized revenue, and future capacity are different observations; track each separately. Company statements below are attributed to the company and are not independent customer or shipment audits.

## Original June issuer cohort

CoreWeave (CRWV), TeraWulf (WULF), IREN, Applied Digital (APLD), Hut 8 (HUT), MARA, CleanSpark (CLSK), Galaxy Digital (GLXY), Nebius (NBIS), Core Scientific (CORZ), Cipher Mining (CIFR), and Bit Digital (BTBT) were the 12-company [June filing sweep](cluster_filing_facts.json). All 12 have current SEC submissions in the September panel. Monitor each next periodic filing for recognized AI/cloud revenue, delivered and billable capacity, customer concentration, capex, debt, and cash interest. Nebius's structured annual facts in the panel remain FY2025, but its [August 12 6-K earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1513845/000110465926094568/tm2622968d1_ex99-1.htm) reports unaudited Q2 consolidated revenue of $582.3 million. CleanSpark's current-period revenue tag is missing, while its [June 30 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/827876/000119312526338382/clsk-20260630.htm) reports $138.006 million of Q2 Bitcoin mining revenue, net. Do not turn those XBRL gaps into zeroes or re-use the June score as a September score.

## Public companies added to the tracked panel

- **Bitdeer (BTDR): promoted from the June financial fixture.** It was already in [the June financial fixture](../handoffs/fixtures/ai_direct_issuer_financials.csv), outside the 12-company filing sweep. The September SEC panel's XBRL selection stops at its [FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1899123/000121390026049770/ea0282628-20f_bitdeer.htm), but an [August 10 6-K earnings exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1899123/000121390026086938/ea030131301ex99-1.htm) reports unaudited Q2 revenue of $228.8 million, including $14.0 million from AI Cloud. Monitor AI Cloud GPUs deployed, externally subscribed GPUs, realized cloud revenue and cash capex; keep future megawatts separate from live capacity.
- **WhiteFiber (WYFI): newly tracked public issuer.** Its [August 12 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2042022/000121390026088026/ea0300877-10q_whitefiber.htm) reports $29 million Q2 revenue. A $12.3 million customer termination fee was recognized in that quarter; the amended $15.7 million fee remained unpaid when the filing was issued. The company says it redeployed the affected GPUs. Monitor collection of that fee and recurring revenue from the redeployed GPUs before using Q2 revenue as a run rate. Bit Digital owns 69.6% of WhiteFiber as of that filing; their financial statements overlap. Both report the same $344.712 million H1 cash capex, so do not sum parent and subsidiary figures.
- **SpaceX (SPCX): newly tracked public demand counterparty.** Its [August 4 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm) provides a filed demand-side balance sheet: $7.814 billion Q2 consolidated revenue and $38.433 billion on the selected debt tag. Monitor future filings for AI-compute commitments, delivered compute, cash payments and the share of demand funded by SpaceX itself.
- **AMD: newly tracked chip and leasing counterparty.** Its [August 5 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2488/000000248826000123/amd-20260627.htm) supplies filed revenue and debt for a second chip vendor. [Core Scientific's Q2 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839341/000183934126000014/core-20260630.htm) records 377 MW of signed AMD leases and a separate 1,925 MW capacity reservation right. Monitor realized AI accelerator sales, financing exposure, and the signed capacity that becomes billable.

NVIDIA, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle were already in the repo's [public EDGAR seeds](../src/bubble/ingestion/edgar/seeds.py). Their September filings are refreshed in the 22-CIK snapshot. Track their actual AI revenue and capex against the weaker issuers' signed capacity; do not count the same hyperscaler spend as independent end-demand at every layer.

Oracle's [September 11 Q1 fiscal 2027 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341439/000119312526389274/orcl-20260831.htm) reports $23.103 billion operating cash flow and $28.499 billion cash capex for the same quarter. Operating cash flow includes an $11.363 billion increase in deferred revenue from customer prepayments with a significant financing component; financing cash flow includes $19.909 billion of stock issued through its at-the-market program. The $23.103 billion is reported cash flow, but its composition cannot be treated as proof that AI workloads already produced matching earned revenue.

SpaceX demand cross-check: In [June 1 SEC correspondence](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026039278/filename1.htm), the staff asked SpaceX to quantify the compute covered by Anthropic's $1.25 billion monthly fee. SpaceX's [final prospectus filed June 12](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026042639/spaceexplorationtechnologi.htm) specifies approximately 325,000 NVIDIA GPUs across COLOSSUS and COLOSSUS II, reduced fees during the May-June ramp, and either party's right to terminate on 90 days' notice after the initial three months. The prospectus says the two clusters collectively provide about 1.0 GW, but it gives no MW allocated to Anthropic. Annualizing the steady-state fee yields $15 billion of conditional infrastructure rent. It cannot establish $50 billion of model inference revenue per GW: the allocated MW denominator is unavailable, and customer cloud spend is a different metric from the customer's model revenue. The [August 4 Q2 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm) recognizes reserved-capacity revenue as capacity becomes available and reports $2.194 billion of Q2 AI Solutions & Infrastructure revenue. That line also includes subscriptions, data licensing, and API access; the filing does not identify Anthropic's recognized revenue. An unnamed customer represented 19.5% of consolidated Q2 revenue, all attributable to the AI segment. SpaceX describes Anthropic as a third-party compute customer; its own Grok training and inference use is separate from that external contract.

The June [SpaceX adjacency card](spacex_adjacency.md) withdrew a Google compute contract after checking the final prospectus but missing a separate [June 5 SEC free-writing prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026041150/spacexagreementfwp.htm). That primary filing confirms a Google agreement for approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs and $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029 after a reduced-fee ramp. Google can terminate or reduce fees if GPUs are not delivered after a grace period, and either side may terminate with 90 days' notice after December 31, 2026. The June card's withdrawal is therefore superseded. The future fee is not Q2 recognized revenue. Separately, the [June 1 SEC correspondence](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026039278/filename1.htm) discloses one unnamed secondary-offering investor with debt and Grok-subscription arrangements; SpaceX said its $20 million investment represented about 2% of that offering and had the same price as investors without those commercial ties. The letter does not identify Anthropic as that investor.

SpaceX's [August 4 10-Q, Note 17](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1181412/000162828026052535/spcx-20260630.htm) also reports $13.329 billion of AI-hardware failed-sale-leaseback debt with Valor Equity Partners, whose founder is a SpaceX director, and $327 million of related interest expense for Q2 2026 ($513 million for H1). The debt comprises $2.039 billion current and $11.290 billion noncurrent, already included in the $38.433 billion total debt above. The June adjacency card's statement that SpaceX had no disclosed AI-infrastructure debt burden is superseded by this Q2 filing. These related-party financing facts concern Valor, not the Anthropic customer agreement.

Demand-side measurement: [OpenAI's September 8 update](https://openai.com/index/the-work-now-within-reach/) reports more than one billion weekly users and 2.5 million businesses. Its [September 8 1Password case](https://openai.com/index/1password/) reports a 20.9% engineering productivity gain and 10.9% lower median pull-request cycle time for its cohort; the 553% ROI and $783,750 annual engineering capacity value are modeled from assumptions including 40% Codex attribution and 75% realization. OpenAI's [September 16 measurement guidance](https://openai.com/index/how-to-connect-ai-usage-to-business-value/) explicitly distinguishes usage from business outcomes. These observations disprove the video's literal claim that nobody measures ROI, but one vendor-published customer case does not establish aggregate end-customer profitability.

## Material watchlist outside the 22-CIK financial panel

These names have primary-source observations but **no cash-flow, debt, or capex refresh in the 22-CIK panel**. Inclusion here is a deliberate tracking decision, not an inference from changes in the SEC ticker universe.

- **SB Energy (proposed SBE): SEC registrant, IPO pending in the filings checked.** Its [August 31 S-1, filed September 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2133037/000162828026059639/sbenergy-sx1.htm), says the initial OpenAI PORTS-Pike lease covers approximately 4.25 GW of IT load over 20 years. NVIDIA's residual-value guarantee has a $105 billion aggregate remedy cap and is conditional on OpenAI default and project milestones; this is potential credit exposure, not current revenue or a payment by NVIDIA. SB Energy says it had generated no data-center revenue as of the prospectus, while its existing power business did have revenue. [NVIDIA's August 26 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) expects the first covered data center ready for service in fiscal 2029. Monitor effective lease commencement, commissioned load, customer payment, financing, and any use of the guarantee.
- **Micron (MU): public HBM and DRAM supplier.** Its [June 25 fiscal Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000015/mu-20260528.htm) reports $41.456 billion quarterly revenue. It attributes much of the year-over-year DRAM and NAND growth to higher selling prices and warns that HBM capacity shifted back to conventional DRAM could create oversupply if HBM demand weakens. Monitor actual HBM revenue and shipments, wafer and packaging capacity, selling prices, and capex against those risks; HBM is not separately reported in that revenue total.
- **SK hynix (SKHY): newly US-listed public HBM supplier.** [Nasdaq's July notice](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/TraderNews.aspx?id=ETA2026-37) set regular-way SKHY trading for July 13. Its [August semi-annual 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526354777/d147827d6k.htm) reports consolidated H1 revenue of KRW 131.895 trillion under K-IFRS. Its [July 29 Q2 release](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/) reports KRW 79.319 trillion quarterly revenue and says HBM4 mass shipments began in Q2; HBM4E had reached sample shipments. Monitor HBM4 volume, pricing, capacity additions, and cash capex separately from the reported total memory revenue.
- **Samsung Electronics: public Korean HBM supplier.** Its [July 30 Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results) report KRW 171.5 trillion consolidated revenue and scaled HBM4 sales, while HBM4E had reached customer samples. Samsung attributes the Memory Business record to AI demand and rising prices. Monitor HBM volume and price, qualification and yield, and committed capacity; no SEC issuer-financial panel refresh was performed for Samsung.
- **TSMC (TSM): public advanced-packaging supplier.** Its [April 23 technology release](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3302) says 5.5-reticle CoWoS is in production, 14-reticle CoWoS integrating about 10 compute dies and 20 HBM stacks is scheduled for production in 2028, and beyond-14-reticle packaging in 2029. It targets production of co-packaged optics in 2026. Track actual production, yield, customer chip shipments, and realized packaging revenue; the 2028-29 technology plan is not 2027 delivered compute.

## Private inference hardware watch

- **Etched:** On [August 18](https://www.etched.com/progress/from-zero-to-one), the company said it shipped its first rack to Jane Street and quoted Jane Street saying the rack was running in its data center. This is a first customer rack, not evidence of volume shipments. Monitor additional customer acceptance, fleet size, utilization, and production inference economics.
- **Positron:** Its [company timeline](https://www.positron.ai/about) says more than 50 Atlas racks were deployed at Oracle in August. Its [September 10 company release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/positron-ai-raises-875-million-at-a-5-billion-valuation-to-bring-its-next-generation-inference-silicon-to-market-302874601.html) says more than 50 racks were *deploying* and reports an $875 million financing. Treat the rack count as company-reported and deployment status as unsettled between those two statements. Asimov tapeout at end-2026 and Titan production in H2 2027 are plans, not shipments. Monitor Oracle/customer confirmation, live utilization, and Asimov tapeout and volume yield.
- **Fractile:** Its [May company statement](https://www.fractile.ai/news/fractile-raises-220m-to-build-the-next-generation-of-inference-hardware) says it raised $220 million to get its first chips and systems into customers' hands. That statement places customer delivery in the future at the time of publication. Monitor a dated first silicon, system delivery, and customer workload result.
- **MatX:** Its [February 24 announcement](https://matx.com/research/series_b) describes a $500 million Series B to finish MatX One and targets tapeout within a year. Its [product page](https://matx.com/) states performance claims but provides no dated customer shipment in the pages checked. Monitor completed tapeout, working silicon, customer delivery, and measured throughput on specified workloads.

Private-company funding and performance claims are not recognized revenue or independently measured output. The video is a lead to verify, not the evidence for these entries.

## Flexible-load infrastructure added September 16

- **Emerald AI:** [NVIDIA's September 16 announcement](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-energy-management-alliance/) identifies Emerald AI, Google, and NVIDIA as founders of the AI Energy Management Alliance. The coalition seeks utility and regulatory acceptance of data centers that can reduce their electricity use when the grid requests it; the announcement does not quantify additional grid capacity approved or delivered. [NVIDIA's September 15 account](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/from-megawatts-to-tokens-how-nvidia-maximizes-ai-factory-production/) reports a Silicon Valley Power field event in which a site reduced draw from 4 MW to 3 MW while higher-priority workloads continued. That is a vendor-described local demonstration, not evidence that the same fraction applies to all training or inference workloads. Monitor independently metered curtailment, service quality, signed flexible-load tariffs, connection approvals, and repeatable customer economics. [Emerald's August 25 financing announcement](https://www.emeraldai.co/blog/emerald-ai-raises-150-million-series-a) reports a $150 million Series A, which is financing, not revenue.
