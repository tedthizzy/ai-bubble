# State utility and large-load source audit, September 16, 2026

The [machine-readable manifest](state_utility_source_audit_2026-09-16_v2.json) lists official source-system URLs for all 50 states and DC, the HTTP probe result for each, 21 retained raw acquisition artifacts with hashes, and definitions for every quantitative measure below. This is a source-system census, not a complete project or metered-load census. The [original NARUC regulator roster](https://www.naruc.org/about-naruc/our-mission/regulatory-commissions/) supplied 51 state/DC regulator records. All 51 roster pages downloaded. We found 50 commission homepage URLs in those rosters; NJ was added from its [official BPU site](https://www.nj.gov/bpu/). Commission homepages returned HTTP 200 for 43 of 50 attempts; AK, CO, MI, and NH returned 403, OH returned 404, and MT and TX failed TLS verification. Direct public-case/source-system probes returned HTTP 200 for 47 of 51 states/DC, 403 for AK/NC/NH, and a TLS verification error for TX. These are retrieval failures, not claims that records are absent.

The strongest direct-load evidence is regional and uses different populations:

- [ERCOT August 2026 Monthly Operational Overview](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/16/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-August-2026.pdf), PDF p9, SHA256 `f02f0b38492f73bc64fdf938a1f625e72fbfc0a9ab8e98508b1125be5957b7e8`: 9,456 MW of large loads had Approval to Energize. ERCOT observed 4,316 MW of August *non-simultaneous monthly peak consumption*, the sum of each load's monthly maximum. The [July report](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/08/17/ERCOT-Monthly-Operational-Overview-July-2026.pdf), PDF p10, reported 4,370 MW using the same definition. The August figure is 54 MW lower. It is neither an instantaneous grid peak nor average load. The slides do not split observed MW among data centers, crypto miners, and other large loads.
- [ERCOT revised February System Operations Update](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/02/04/9.3-System-Operations-Update-REVISED.pdf), PDF p16, SHA256 `df78d6acb92f8b68d50e419a16a80fc614d513781b1cd8b9ceaa6b61757fdeb5`: a separate set of about 400 operational cloud/storage/colocation/AI data-center sites at least 1 MW, excluding crypto, had 9,850 MW of installed or approved capacity. ERCOT says their actual MW grew 15.5% from January 2025 to January 2026. The chart shows roughly 3.6 GW average daily load in January 2026; the precise daily series is not in this PDF. This cohort overlaps the broader ERCOT large-load cohort and must not be added to it.
- [California Energy Commission Data Center Methodology Memo](https://www.energy.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/Data_Center_Methodology_Memo_ada.pdf), PDF p2 and p6, SHA256 `06b6449e84f2281a25eaf92a099e3a22e08943c0f2ba23c0354a5747b10a2842`: approximately 1,000 MW of existing California data-center peak demand as of December 2025. Seven utilities reported 23,278 MW of requested service capacity: 5,086 MW signed agreements, 9,587 MW active applications, and 8,604 MW inquiries. The published stage values sum to 23,277 MW, a 1 MW rounding difference. The total includes 2,600 MW of Valley Electric Association applications for *Nevada* sites within CAISO. It is not a California-sited project total. PDF pp8-9 applies a 67% maximum-demand/request-capacity factor, based on the upper end of observed Silicon Valley Power experience, and completion assumptions of 70%/33%/0% for signed/application/inquiry in the Planning Forecast. PDF p11's 85-90% observed hourly load factor uses *observed annual maximum demand* as its denominator, a different ratio. The companion [Form 1.1c workbook](https://efiling.energy.ca.gov/GetDocument.aspx?tn=268824), SHA256 `aa1720541d2ea08f5beeda8e7de7aed50269aee24ca9be9d32c4b17ee4cfb1c2`, forecasts incremental data-center GWh; its statewide total row 103 is zero despite nonzero utility rows, so the statewide cells are unusable.
- [PJM May 2026 Load Forecast Accuracy Report](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/load-forecast/2026-load-forecast-accuracy-report.pdf), PDF p3, and [data workbook](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/res-adeq/load-forecast/2026-accuracy-report.xlsx), `RTO!D15`, `RTO!D18`, and `RTO!D19`, SHA256 workbook `e0f24fe03a79af016826089c653b2c2e15e55edcb04a7bd4be5fa643136425fc`: historical July peak for *all large-load adjustments* supplied by EDCs/LSEs increased from 6,135.627 MW in 2024 to 8,167.174 MW in 2025, up 2,031.547 MW or 33.11%; 2025 actual was 432.174 MW above the 7,735 MW forecast. The workbook records historical 2025 zone peaks at `AEP!D82` 1,726.1 MW, `COMED!D55` 622.665 MW, `DOM!D87` 5,563.169 MW, and `PS!D66` 255.24 MW. The PDF's monthly chart is filtered to data centers, while the workbook's annual large-load adjustment series is broader. These MW cannot be called data-center-only or AI IT load. The zone data should not be summed independently because peaks can be noncoincident; use PJM's RTO row.
- [Nebraska Power Review Board 2026 Load and Capability Report](https://powerreview.nebraska.gov/sites/default/files/doc/2026%20Load%20%26%20Capability%20Report%20%28FINAL%29.pdf), PDF pp89 and 93, SHA256 `f511b1413626da7d28663b2107c3d46b9629d7cec12650c2d9c496cc80359f30`: data centers and other computational load, including AI and crypto, total 800-1,100 MW contracted, 350-1,950 MW prospective, and 1,800-4,300 MW speculative. Contracted means signed or similar arrangement expected to be fully interconnected within five years, not current draw. The report warns that speculative inquiries across utility areas may be double counted. Nebraska's electric retail utilities are consumer-owned, so the Power Review Board is the relevant electric source rather than the state Public Service Commission.

Two state sources clarify project stages without supplying delivered MW. The [Minnesota PUC data-center page](https://mn.gov/puc/activities/v-l-e-c/data-centers/) lists Google subsidiaries Harmony Group and Echo Zone ESAs filed March 26 and April 14, 2026, and says customer demand estimates and construction dates may be confidential. The [Georgia PSC fact sheet](https://psc.ga.gov/site/downloads/datacenterfactsheet.pdf), p2, calls 9,985 MW *certified new generation* with about 80% expected for data centers; its [December 19, 2025 press release](https://psc.ga.gov/site/assets/files/9176/media_advisory_dec_19_decision.pdf) says 9,885 MW. Neither number measures operating data-center load. The [ERCOT data-center impact RFI](https://www.ercot.com/about/legal/data-center-impact-rfi) was updated September 14 for developers of at least 25 MW; responses are due October 12 and a report is planned by December 10. No results are available in this as-of snapshot.

The public [EIA Form 861 page](https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia861/) supplied 2025 early-release and 2024 final ZIPs with electric sales, utility frame, and service territories. These establish service coverage and baseline electricity sales. They do not identify data-center electricity use. State PUC dockets, utility agreements, county building permits, state air permits, and ISO/RTO planning reports have different scopes and may conceal customer-specific MW. The 51-state source-system map has not downloaded every docket or every local permit system. No national metered data-center MW total is derived from this pass.

Raw files are retained under `data/source_acquisition_2026-09-16/large_load/raw/state_utility_*`. The manifest records URL, publication date when identifiable, retrieval timestamp, SHA256, byte count, file magic, acquisition status, and the relevant page/cell. A mistaken request to the CEC media landing page returned HTML with a `.pdf` local suffix; the manifest marks that artifact `acquired_wrong_content_type` and identifies the separately downloaded actual PDF. Nothing was removed.
