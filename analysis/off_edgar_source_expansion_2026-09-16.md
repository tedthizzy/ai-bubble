# Off-EDGAR public-source expansion, September 16, 2026 Pacific

This audit records public systems that can test AI-bubble claims outside SEC filings. It separates acquired records from access leads and states the observation date, unit, and limits of each source. Raw files and derived row sets reside locally under `data/off_edgar_expansion_2026-09-16/` and are gitignored; the reusable acquisition scripts are `scripts/parse_public_source_expansion_2026_09_16.py`, `scripts/audit_colorado_ucc_2026_09_16.py`, `scripts/audit_oregon_ucc_2026_09_16.py`, and `scripts/acquire_gdelt_event_day.py`. The counts below are from those local files, not projections.

## Acquired state WARN notices

The [California EDD WARN workbook](https://edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/jobs_and_training/warn/warn_report1.xlsx) contained 220 notice rows for July 1 through September 14, 2026, with 11,669 reported affected workers. That sum reconciles to its summary tab. The newest notice date in the acquired detail was September 11. EDD's [WARN page](https://edd.ca.gov/warn) describes the publication source. The [Texas Workforce Commission 2026 workbook](https://www.twc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/oei/docs/warn-act-listings-2026-twc.xlsx) contained 102 rows, 17,041 reported affected workers, and notices through September 15; its [source page](https://www.twc.texas.gov/data-reports/warn-notice) hosts annual files. [Pennsylvania's 2026 WARN page](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dli/programs-services/workforce-development-home/warn-requirements/warn-notices) contained 56 notice entries and 9,414 reported affected workers. Pennsylvania gives notice month rather than exact date; revisions can overlap prior entries, so 9,414 is a raw entry sum, not a unique worker count.

The normalized `derived/warn_notices.csv` contains all 378 rows, with state, employer, notice timing, affected count, source URL, and workbook row or page entry provenance. `derived/warn_summary.json` stores source hashes and reconciliation totals. The [WARN Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/layoffs/warn) covers planned qualifying employment actions, with state publication differences. These three jurisdictions cannot establish a national layoff rate, realized separations, or AI-caused layoffs. Named observations include California rows for Intel, Salesforce, Apple, Amazon, and Synopsys, but those employer matches alone do not explain why the notices occurred.

For breadth checking, the independent [WARN Data public feed](https://approjects-warn-act-notices.static.hf.space/api.html) yielded 61,330 archived notice IDs across 48 state codes, including 2,700 with 2026 notice dates, on September 16 Pacific. Its raw export and coverage metadata were retained. An audit against the state originals found zero Pennsylvania 2026 rows despite the official 56, Texas ending September 11 despite the official September 15 notice, and only 114 of California's 220 matching the state's employer, affected count, and notice date simultaneously. Two archive records carry future notice dates of October 30 and November 1 despite August first-seen timestamps. The feed's own stale list flags nine states. It is a discovery index, not a reliable national 2026 numerator or substitute for each state's original record.

## Acquired Colorado UCC corpus

Colorado's public [debtor](https://data.colorado.gov/d/8upq-58vz), [filing](https://data.colorado.gov/d/wffy-3uut), and [collateral](https://data.colorado.gov/d/4am6-w6u4) exports were all acquired on September 16. The respective exports contained 2,015,653 debtor rows, 2,592,624 filing rows, and 1,704,752 collateral rows. Each parsed count matched the corresponding public API count before download. Socrata records show source-row refreshes at 09:03:39, 09:08:30, and 09:03:14 UTC, respectively, on September 16. The three files total 1,150,673,033 bytes; their individual byte counts and SHA-256 hashes are in `derived/ucc_summary.json`. These are three record types with many-to-many links, not 6.31 million separate liens or borrowers. None of their exposed columns carries a loan amount, so a full-corpus scan cannot apply the repo's $1 million materiality threshold. That limit holds whether or not names are filtered by AI topic.

A broad name-pattern join produced 69 debtor rows across 68 file IDs, including 41 Crusoe, 20 Flexential, five EdgeCore, and two QTS pattern matches. The single `Applied Digital` match is a different, older name: `APPLIED DIGITAL DATA SYSTEMS INC`, filed in 1992. The Crusoe matched filing dates run from 2020 through April 23, 2025; this pass found no 2026 Crusoe filing. These are unverified legal-entity leads, often historical or inactive. UCC notice does not disclose current outstanding debt or show distress. The raw exports include individual names and addresses, so raw records and candidate details remain out of git; publish only audited aggregates or specifically verified corporate records.

Oregon's public [prior-month UCC filings](https://data.oregon.gov/d/snfi-f79b) and [current secured-party list](https://data.oregon.gov/d/2kf7-i54h) were also acquired. The former is an August 1-31, 2026 slice with 5,738 party rows and 2,585 distinct lien numbers; the latter has 220,136 secured-party rows and 214,875 distinct filing numbers, with filing dates through September 1, 2026. Both source datasets were last row-refreshed September 2, and both parsed counts matched official API totals. `derived/oregon_ucc_summary.json` records file sizes, SHA-256 hashes, schema, date limits, and lien-type counts. The first file does not cover historic Oregon debtors; the second has no debtor-name or amount column. Neither can supply a state-wide $1 million exposure numerator.

Additional official bulk routes were mapped but not acquired: [California's UCC portal manual](https://bpd.cdn.sos.ca.gov/ucc/ucc-online-help.pdf) describes free weekly UCC data and images behind portal login and a $100 master unload; [Kentucky's bulk service](https://www.sos.ky.gov/bus/Pages/Bulk-Data-Service.aspx) describes full monthly and weekly UCC files under a subscriber agreement, with noncommercial users exempt from the monthly fee; [Texas's bulk file layouts](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/ucc/bulk-order-file-layouts.shtml) describe monthly master and daily update orders available on purchase; and [Idaho's UCC data page](https://sos.idaho.gov/ucc/) describes biweekly extracts at $125 per file. No account, purchase, or billable endpoint was used for these leads.

## Insurer investment exposure

The [NAIC second-quarter 2026 industry snapshots](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/2026-2q-snapshots.pdf), covering June 30 statements filed by August 18, were acquired. Life and health insurers reported $143.646 billion first-half net investment income, versus $131.780 billion a year earlier, and $22.832 billion operating income, versus $25.961 billion. The [NAIC 2025 life industry commentary](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/2025-annual-life-industry-commentary.pdf) reports $5,971.6 billion of year-end invested assets, including $3,999.7 billion bonds and $832.8 billion mortgage loans. The 2026 snapshot is aggregate; the investment allocation is year-end 2025. Neither identifies an individual insurer's AI or data-center credit holdings.

The [NAIC statutory financial-data catalog](https://content.naic.org/prod_serv_financial_home.htm) offers company-level InsData under purchase and licensing terms. No paid request was made. The [California Insurance Commissioner's 2025 annual report](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0200-studies-reports/0700-commissioner-report/upload/2025-Annual-Report-of-the-Commissioner.pdf) was also acquired and checked; it reports regulatory operations and selected financial information, not an issuer-level AI credit holdings panel. Other state access leads need separate issuer-level acquisition and reconciliation: the [New York DFS supplement public-access portal](https://myportal-t.dfs.ny.gov/web/guest-applications/nysupp-public-access) provides state supplements, and [New Jersey's 2025 domestic health insurer statements](https://www.nj.gov/dobi/division_insurance/solvency/annualstatements/2025/wellcarehealthins/index.html) are company pages. These alternatives do not yet provide a harmonized September 2026 insurer portfolio panel in this repo.

## Utility large-load requests and planned supply

[Dominion's February 2, 2026 Virginia State Corporation Commission application, Case PUR-2026-00011](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch/DOCS/8%40%23101%21.PDF) reported, as of December 31, 2025, roughly 25,000 MW of large-load distribution-planning requests with projected connection dates through 2031 and another roughly 45,000 MW batched for study. The gross 70,000 MW request queue was nearly three times the company's 24,678 MW historical zone peak. Dominion said about ten new requests totaling 2,000-3,000 MW were arriving per month at the time. These are the utility's dated statements about requests, not energized capacity or a unique AI-only load count. The comparison to historic peak shows scale, not a forecast of incremental power consumption. The filing PDF was acquired locally.

The acquired [Georgia Public Service Commission 2025 annual report](https://psc.ga.gov/site/downloads/annual_reports/2025_GAPSC_Annual_Report.pdf) says its December 30, 2025 orders in Dockets 56298 and 56310 certified a 9,885 MW resource portfolio for projected 2028-2031 needs, driven mainly by data-center growth. That is approved generation-resource planning, not a 2025 or 2026 connected data-center load figure. [Other state utility source checks](state_utility_source_audit_2026-09-16.md) cover distinct ERCOT, California, and Missouri signals. Queue requests, resource approvals, projected loads, and live metered demand need separate denominators; adding them creates false national demand.

## Patent source access

The [USPTO PatentsView page](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/economic-research/patentsview), updated June 23, 2026, describes downloadable research datasets through December 31, 2025. That is a lagged research extract, not current issued-patent evidence. The [USPTO Open Data Portal bulk-data interface](https://data.uspto.gov/apis/bulk-data/search) says registration and an API key are required from June 18, 2026. An unauthenticated request to its dataset endpoint returned HTTP 403 `MissingAuthenticationTokenException`; a legacy PatentsView S3 bulk URL also returned HTTP 403 during this pass. No patent records were acquired here. The gap is access to a current official patent corpus, not evidence that companies filed no patents.

## GDELT news-event acquisition and boundary

The free [GDELT 2.0 bulk event files](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gdeltv2/lastupdate.txt) were acquired and parsed across all 96 quarter-hour UTC slots on September 16: 116,857 machine-coded event rows. The seven available September 17 UTC slots from 00:00 through 01:30 added 9,986 event rows. There were zero failed slots, 126,843 distinct event IDs, and 25,746 distinct original `SOURCEURL` values across both slices. Each derived row retains its original first-report URL, GDELT event ID, event/date-added fields, and raw-file provenance. The [GDELT event codebook](https://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf) defines the fields. Summary manifests reside in `gdelt_2026-09-16/acquisition_summary.json` and `gdelt_2026-09-17_partial/acquisition_summary.json` under the local data directory.

The full September 16 UTC slice includes September 15 Pacific evening and excludes September 16 Pacific evening. The partial September 17 UTC slice extends observation through September 16 at 6:30 p.m. Pacific; later Pacific evening was unavailable at run time. `SOURCEURL` values repeat, may point to syndication, and do not establish independent reporting. GDELT event rows are coded media observations across all subjects, not a count of AI articles or confirmed economic outcomes. The event's historical date can precede its file's publication slot. The older `scripts/gdelt_news_distress.py` uses a rolling six-month DOC API query and article-count threshold without retaining URLs; its June report should not be treated as a reproducible September corroboration. The DOC API returned HTTP 429 on two attempts during this run, while bulk acquisition succeeded.

## What the coverage supports

These additions provide independently retrievable primary state notices, one complete state UCC corpus plus two Oregon UCC extracts, current aggregate insurer statements, dated utility request and resource filings, and a reproducible daily GDELT event snapshot. They do not yield a national WARN series, dollar-valued all-state UCC collateral exposure, insurer-level AI credit holdings, September 2026 patents, or measured conversion of utility requests into operational megawatts. No conclusion about the size or timing of an AI bubble should treat those absent measures as zeros.
