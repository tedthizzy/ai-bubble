Two stylized inversions, each reported as a **sensitivity band** across a scenario grid (discount rates {10%, 12%, 15%} × steady-state FCF margins {15%, 25%, 35%}; terminal growth 4% for the revenue frame):

1. **Implied revenue multiple** — via a Gordon-growth perpetuity, the perpetual revenue the EV requires, as a multiple of current revenue. *The price implies revenue must reach ~Nx today's and hold there forever.*
2. **Renewal-dependent share of EV** — the share of EV the *contracted* backlog cannot justify on its own (PV of backlog-derived FCF over its tenor), which therefore rests on **re-contracting the assets** after the first term. This is the inversion that bites: a high share is the market betting GPUs get re-leased at healthy rates after their first contract — against the engine's ~2–3yr GPU economic life vs 5–7yr debt.

## Reading it

- **The revenue multiple is the size of the growth bet.** Even on generous steady-state margins, the price asks these names to multiply revenue several-fold and hold it in perpetuity. Where the band's low end is already a large multiple, the optimism is not a tail scenario — it is the base case the price requires.
- **The renewal-dependent share is the size of the GPU-life bet.** It answers: once the signed contracts run off, how much of today's price still needs paying? For the GPU clouds that share is high *and* lands on assets the engine measures at ~2–3yr economic life — the market is pricing re-lease economics the collateral may not live to deliver.
- **The landlord is not the safe contrast you'd expect.** Even APLD — a data-center *landlord* with long leases — lands in the same high renewal-dependent band as the GPU clouds. Under equal FCF margins a 12-year lease spreads rent thin per year and discounts it heavily, so a long tenor does *not* mechanically cover more of the EV (the model disproves the intuition). A landlord's true lease margin is higher than this grid assumes, which would lower its share — carded below as a limitation — but even granting it, ~$11B of APLD's $16B backlog is CoreWeave, so its backlog quality is only as good as a GPU cloud's. The recursion doesn't remove the risk; it relocates it.

## What this does NOT capture (carded, not hidden)

- Backlog revenue is spread **evenly** over its tenor (real recognition is front- or back-loaded); capex is **already sunk** into EV via debt; current revenue and near-term backlog may **double-count**; capacity **option value** is ignored. These are comparative instruments across names and against the engine's measurements, **not valuations**.
- `current_annualized_revenue` basis **differs per name** (stated in the per-name notes) because these are mid-ramp businesses; the direction each choice pushes the multiple is noted so a hostile reader can re-run their own basis.
- **A single FCF-margin grid is applied to all four**, including the landlord (APLD), whose real lease margin is structurally higher than a GPU cloud's. That uniform grid *overstates* APLD's renewal-dependent share; a landlord-appropriate margin would lower it. The grid is kept uniform for comparability and the bias is flagged rather than silently corrected.
- The scenario grid is wide but finite; the published `viz/expectations.json` carries the full per-name inputs and bands for independent re-computation.
