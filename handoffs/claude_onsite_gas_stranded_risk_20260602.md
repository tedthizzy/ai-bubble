## The blind spot
The verified physical research surfaced that the flagship AI-DC projects are building **enormous dedicated on-site
gas generation** — and **the off-grid portion sits in NO surface the engine measures**: it's not SEC debt (so not in
the $3.742T metric), not in the grid module (no ISO queue, by design), and not ratepayer (off-grid has no rate base).

**~7.7 GW of new gas generation is being built specifically for these AI datacenters; ~5.4 GW of it is OFF-GRID and
invisible to every engine surface.**

## Why it's a real bubble risk (three layers)
1. **Stranded-asset risk:** an off-grid gas plant built for ONE datacenter has **no alternative offtake** if that DC's
   demand fails to materialize (the whole point of off-grid is dedication). Adams Fork's >4.8 GW — larger than all but
   two existing WV power plants — is a single-customer bet. If the AI-compute cycle cracks, these plants strand with no
   grid to absorb them.
2. **Litigation / permit risk (already crystallizing):** TransGas faces a **federal lawsuit** (Dec 2025); **xAI ran
   turbines WITHOUT an air permit** → Clean Air Act suit (NAACP v. xAI). These are active, not hypothetical.
3. **The Meta/Richland exception proves the rule:** when the gas IS grid-connected (utility-built), the stranded-asset
   risk transfers to **ratepayers** (my #31 lane). Off-grid keeps it on developer/JV equity + project lenders — a
   different, un-tracked bearer.

## Proposed treatment
Add an **on-site-generation hidden-risk surface** (separate from the debt metric and the grid module): per project,
the dedicated MW, fuel, off-grid-vs-grid, the stranded-asset bearer, and litigation status. It answers a question the
current engine cannot: *"how much power-generation capex is being built as a single-customer bet on AI demand, and
who eats it if the demand isn't there?"* Sourced from state air-permit dockets (the same handles as my physical cards).
