## The chain
secured-SPV debt (depreciating-GPU collateral) → serviceable only from customer-contract cash flow → so the whole
edifice rests on **how durable those contracts are.** For CoreWeave (the round-trip hub, best-disclosed):

## CoreWeave contracted backlog — take-or-pay with TWO holes
- **96% of revenue is multi-year locked-in take-or-pay** — customers pay whether or not they use the compute. Durable
  against convenience-cancellation. GOOD.
- **Hole 1 — the nonperformance carve-out:** contracts require payment to end of lease **"except in case of
  nonperformance."** If CoreWeave can't deliver capacity (construction delays), **customers can cancel** (explicit risk
  factor). There is **active securities litigation about construction-timeline delays** — i.e. the carve-out is a live
  risk, and it bites exactly when CoreWeave is operationally stressed (the worst time to lose the take-or-pay shield).
- **Hole 2 — counterparty concentration & credit:** **Microsoft 62%/67%/71%** of revenue (2024/2025/Q2'25); **OpenAI
  $22.4B** total commitment through Oct-2030 ($11.9B + $4B + expansions). Take-or-pay is only as good as the payer —
  and OpenAI is itself deeply unprofitable ("what if the AI company can't pay up?"). Single-name concentration means one
  counterparty failure breaks the cash flow that services the debt.

## Why this is the thesis crux
The bull case is "96% contracted take-or-pay backlog = durable revenue." The forensic reality: that backlog is
**conditional on CoreWeave's own delivery (nonperformance carve-out, under litigation) AND on a handful of concentrated,
partly-unprofitable counterparties.** So the contracted revenue that underwrites the secured debt is **neither
unconditional nor diversified** — the two classic durability failure modes are both present and both currently live.
